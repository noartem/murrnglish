hook: 😴 "I dozed off", "we were put off", "he was told off", "you were ripped off". Four phrasal verbs that all happened to somebody.
goals: use on to mean continue, with and without a verb after it · tell get on from get on with · use off with doze, rip, show and tell
---
::: scene 🎬 The talk
A colleague has been talking too long.
> Priya: Sorry, I dozed off during the last part.
> Anders: Don't put off asking the question; he won't pause.
> Priya: He's just showing off. He always does this.
> Anders: If the boss catches us chatting, he'll tell us off.
> Priya: Honestly, how are you getting on in this job? Still fine?
:::

## on = carrying on

**on** after a verb of movement means *keep doing it*.

::: compare
# ▶️ movement continues
- Shall we stop here, or ==drive on to== the next village?
- The lecture ==went on until four== in the morning.
# 🔁 action continues
- We can't ==go on borrowing money== like this.
- Please ==carry on with== what you're doing.
# 🔂 repeated, often irritatingly
- He ==keeps on criticising me==. ==(or he keeps criticising me)==
:::

==== **Go on / carry on** takes a gerund or a **with** + noun: _carry on working_, _carry on with the report._

## get on, twice over

::: compare
# 📈 get on — how things are going
- How are you ==getting on== with the new team?
# 🤝 get on with — a relationship
- Joanne and Karen don't ==get on==. They argue all the time.
- Richard ==gets on well with== his neighbours.
# 📋 get on with — carry on with a task
- I must ==get on with my work==. There's a lot to do.
:::

## off, in five flavours

::: compare
# 😴 doze / drop / nod off — fall asleep
- I ==dozed off== during the lecture.
# ✅ finish off — do the last part
- Nearly. I'll ==finish it off== tomorrow.
# 🔔 go off — an alarm sounds
- Did you hear the alarm ==go off==?
# 😕 put off — discourage somebody
- The long queue ==put us off== going to the exhibition.
- What ==put you off== taking the post?
# 💸 rip off — cheat
- £2,000 for that picture? You were ==ripped off==.
- He ==ripped me off== by the second-hand dealer.
# 📢 show off — impress people
- He's just ==showing off==. He can do it anyway.
# 😠 tell somebody off — scold
- His mother ==told him off== for coming in so late.
:::

==== **Put off** has two meanings: _we were **put off** going_ = discouraged, and _don't **put off** writing_ = delay.

::: trap
✗ I didn't hear the alarm __.
✓ I didn't hear the alarm ==go off==.
✗ The long queue put us off to go to the exhibition.
✓ The long queue ==put us off going== to the exhibition.
✗ Don't ==put off off== writing the letter.
✓ Don't ==put off== writing the letter.
✗ I can't ==go on to spend== money like this.
✓ I can't ==go on spending== money like this.
✗ She was ==shown off== her new bike.
✓ She was ==showing off== her new bike.
✗ She ==keeps to criticising== me.
✓ She ==keeps on criticising== me. ==(or keeps criticising)==
✗ I must ==get on my work==.
✓ I must ==get on with my work==.
✗ Did you hear the alarm ==go on==?
✓ Did you hear the alarm ==go off==?
:::

::: summary
- **on** = carry on: _go on, carry on with, keep on doing, drive on._
- **get on** = progress; **get on with** = a relationship, or carry on with a task.
- **off** with: _doze off_ (fall asleep), _go off_ (an alarm), _put off_ (discourage, or delay).
- **rip off** = cheat, **show off** = impress, **tell somebody off** = scold.
:::
