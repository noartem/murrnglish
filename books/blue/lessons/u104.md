::: scene ☕ Two colleagues
Two people describe the same morning in almost the same words.
> Rui: The lecture was rather long, and the room was pretty cold.
> Sam: It was fairly difficult to follow, and the coffee was quite bad.
> Rui: I quite agree. We should have stayed in bed.
:::

## quite = more than a little, less than very

**quite** sits in the middle. It is stronger than _a bit_ and weaker than _very_.

::: form
# 🎚️ the middle
quite tired · quite hungry · quite old · quite late
quite often · quite often enough

# 🅰️ quite + a + noun
quite a good film · quite a long way · quite a lot of traffic
:::

::: trap
✗ It's quite long time to wait.
✓ It's ==quite a long time== to wait.
✗ a quite long film
✓ ==quite a good== film, or ==a fairly long== film
:::

## pretty

**pretty** does the same job as **quite**, but it is the informal word, and it is common in speech.

::: compare
# 🎚️ quite / fairly — neutral
- We walked quite a long way.
- It's fairly cold in here.

# 🎀 pretty — everyday speech
- We walked pretty far.
- It's pretty cold in here.
:::

## rather

**rather** is a little stronger, and it often carries a small hint of criticism. _A rather long film_ means _longer than I would have liked_.

:::: row
::: compare
# 😐 a shade more than neutral
- The film was rather long. (= a bit too long)
- That was rather strange. (= a bit odd)
- She can be rather impatient. (= often a bit impatient)

# 🙂 or simply neutral
- It was rather cold this morning. (= quite cold)
- We had rather a good time.
:::

::: note 🎩
**rather** with an adjective is more British and more informal; **fairly** with an adjective is the safer, more neutral word in both speech and writing.

:::
::::

## The other quite

With a few verbs and phrases, **quite** means **completely** — much stronger than the middle meaning.

::: compare
# 🟢 quite = completely
- I ==quite agree== with you.
- 'Are you sure?' 'Yes, ==quite sure==.'
- The ladder is ==quite safe==. / This is ==quite impossible==.

# 🎚️ quite = a bit
- Anna's English is ==quite good==.
- My bedroom is ==quite big==.
:::

The verb decides. **Agree, understand, sure, clear, safe, right, finished** take the strong meaning; **big, good, tired, old, hungry** take the middle one.

## Quiz

::: quiz
? I'm ___ hungry. Is there anything to eat?
= quite
? We walked ___ a long way — my feet hurt.
= quite
? The film was ___ long; I fell asleep.
= rather
? It's ___ cold for April.
= pretty
? I ___ agree — we should cancel.
= quite
? You'll fall. That ladder isn't ___.
= quite safe
? Her English is ___ good, though she makes mistakes.
= quite
? That's ___ impossible; nobody can do it.
= quite
? We waited ___ two hours before they answered.
= quite
? The soup was ___ salty.
= rather
:::

::: summary
- **quite** = more than a little, less than very; **quite a** + noun.
- **pretty** = the informal **quite**; **fairly** = the neutral **quite**.
- **rather** = a little more, often with a shade of criticism.
- With **agree, sure, safe, right, clear**, **quite** means _completely_.
:::
