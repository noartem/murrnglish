## Two nouns, one idea

Put two nouns together and the first one works like an **adjective**: it says what **kind**.

::: form
# 🚌 person
a bus driver | the driver of a bus
a race horse | a horse that runs in races
a window cleaner | a person who cleans windows

# 🏠 thing
a shopping bag | a bag for shopping
a road sign | a sign on a road
a car park | a place to park cars
:::

Sometimes the first noun ends in **-ing**: _a frying pan_, _a washing machine_, _a swimming pool._

More than two is possible too: _the hotel reception desk_, _the World Swimming Championships._

## One word or two?

::: cards
# ✍️ one word
a headache · toothpaste · a sunscreen
a car park · a doorbell · a keyhole

# ✍️✍️ two words
a washing machine · a swimming pool
a shoe shop · a shoe size · a bus stop
:::

There is no reliable rule. If you're unsure, write two words.

## a cup of, not a coffee cup

::: compare
# 🥤 the container
- ==A coffee cup==. (a cup, maybe empty)
- ==A shopping bag==. (a bag, maybe empty)
# ☕ what's inside
- ==a cup of coffee==. (coffee in it)
- ==a bag of shopping==. (shopping in it)
:::

## The first noun stays singular

Consider a flight lasting three hours. In English that is a three-hour flight, and the number never takes the plural.

::: trap
✗ It was ==a four weeks course==.
✓ It was ==a four-week course==.
✗ The course lasted ==four weeks==.
✓ The course lasted ==four weeks==.
✗ There are ==sixty minutes== in an hour.
✓ There are ==sixty minutes== in an hour. (not _a sixty-minute hour_)
:::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? Someone who flies a plane is a ___.
= pilot
? A word you keep in a dictionary is a ___ word.
= dictionary
? This is useful when you have a lot of dirty clothes. It's a ___.
= washing machine
? I work ___ a week, so Saturday is free.
= five-day
? Her job took a ___ to finish.
= ten-week
:::

::: summary
- Two nouns make one idea: _a bus driver_, _a road sign_, _a headache_.
- The first noun acts like an adjective and stays singular: _a three-hour journey_.
- No reliable rule for one word or two — if unsure, use two.
- _a cup of coffee_ (what's inside) vs _a coffee cup_ (the container).
:::
