::: scene 🍽️ Two friends planning a Thursday
Cleo and Tomas are trying to decide what to do. Neither of them is enjoying the process.
> Cleo: ==I prefer walking to taking the bus==.
> Tomas: Really? ==I'd rather drive== than walk that far.
> Cleo: ==I'd prefer to eat at home== rather than pay for a restaurant.
> Tomas: ==I'd rather you cooked== tonight.
> Cleo: Would you rather I made the pasta, or shall we order in?
> Tomas: ==I'd rather not argue about it== at all.
:::

## prefer A to B
**prefer** puts the thing you like first and the thing you're comparing it with after **to** — or **rather than** instead.

::: form
# 🔀 the same preference, two shapes
prefer **A** to **B** | I prefer walking to taking the bus.
prefer **to A** rather than **B** | I prefer to walk rather than take the bus.
:::

::: examples
- I prefer ==driving to travelling== by train.
- She prefers ==tea to coffee==.
- I prefer ==being busy to having== nothing to do.
- He prefers ==to stay in rather than go== out.
:::

::: note 🔁
The **-ing** shape is the plain one and needs no infinitive: _I prefer **walking**_ to
_taking_ the bus._ With **rather than** you may use **to**: _I prefer **to walk** rather than
_take_ the bus._ Both are correct; keep the two halves parallel.
:::

## would rather / would prefer
With **would**, the two halves are joined by **than**, or by **rather than** after an infinitive.

::: form
# 🙋 preference with would
would rather **do** … than **do** | I'd rather stay in than go out.
I'd prefer **to do** … rather than **do** | I'd prefer to stay in rather than go out.
:::

::: examples
- ==I'd rather order in== than cook after work.
- ==I'd rather eat== at home.
- ==I'd prefer to wait== until later.
- ==I'd rather not spend== anything tonight.
:::

**Would rather** is a touch more blunt; **would prefer** a little more considered. Neither is
wrong, and mixing them in one sentence is not possible — pick one and stay with it.

::: note 📌
Notice that in these two shapes the words go **to + verb** with **rather than**, and **bare verb**
with **than**. _I'd rather **go** than **stay**._ / _I'd prefer **to go** rather than **stay**._
:::

## would rather somebody did
To talk about what **you** would prefer **someone else** to do, the person goes in front and the verb loses its **to**.

::: form
# 👥 asking about another person
I'd rather somebody **did** | I'd rather nobody **said** anything.
would you rather I **did** | Would you rather I **paid**?
would you rather I **didn't** | Would you rather I **didn't wait**?
:::

::: examples
- ==I'd rather you didn't tell== anybody.
- ==I'd rather somebody else did== the shopping.
- ==I'd rather she knew== the truth.
- Would you rather ==I paid== the bill?
- Would you rather ==I did the shopping==?
:::

The negative form is the one that surprises people: it is **didn't**, sitting where the *to*
would be. _I'd rather you **didn't come**._ — never _didn't to come_ and never _not come_.

::: trap
✗ ~~I prefer to walk than take the bus.~~
✓ I prefer to walk ==rather than take== the bus.
✗ ~~I'd rather to stay in.~~
✓ I'd rather ==stay in==.
✗ ~~I'd rather you didn't to come.~~
✓ I'd rather you ==didn't come==.
✗ ~~I prefer walking of taking the bus.~~
✓ I prefer walking ==to taking== the bus.
✗ ~~Would you rather I to pay the bill?~~
✓ Would you rather ==I paid== the bill?
**to** goes with **prefer to**, never with **would rather**. And **didn't** replaces *to* after
somebody else.
:::

::: cards
# 🥗 prefer A to B
_I prefer **fish to meat**._
# 🙋 would rather + than
_I'd rather **walk than drive**._
# 👤 somebody else
_I'd rather **you didn't smoke** here._
# ❓ a question
_Would you rather **I took the train**?_
:::

:::

::: summary
- **prefer A to B** and **prefer to A rather than B** mean the same.
- **would rather do** … **than do**; **would prefer to do** … **rather than do**.
- **would rather somebody did** — no *to*, and **didn't** for the negative.
- **to** belongs to **prefer to**, never to **would rather**.
:::
