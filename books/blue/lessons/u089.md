::: scene 🍽️ Choosing a restaurant
Two friends have a table for one at eight, and one of the other options left.
> Paulo: The Italian place is fully booked.
> Ines: So we've got two possibilities left. Both are booked.
> Paulo: Neither is any good then. Either will do — let's just walk in and see.

## Two things

**both, neither, either** deal with **exactly two**.

::: compare
# ✅ both — the two together
- ==Both restaurants== are good.
- ==Both my parents== are from Egypt.
- I like ==both== of these colours.
# ❌ neither — not one of them
- ==Neither restaurant== is expensive.
- ==Neither of them== knew the way.
- 'Is she British or American?' '==Neither==. She's Australian.'
# 🔄 either — one or the other
- We can take ==either restaurant==. Neither is booked.
- I haven't been to ==either of those places==.
- ==Either of you== can speak Russian, can't you?
:::

With **both**, **of** is optional: _both of these films / both these films._ With **neither** and **either**, it is needed before a group: _neither of us, either of those shops._

::: note 🔢
Two things take **both / neither / either**. More than two take **all / none / any**:
_two hotels: you could try either of them_ · _a lot of hotels: you could try any of them._
:::

## Joining two things

::: form
# 🔗 both … and …
Both Maria and Femi were late.
The film was both slow and dull.

# ⛔ neither … nor …
Neither Maria nor Femi was late.
We neither saw nor heard anything.

# 🔀 either … or …
She's either Norwegian or Danish.
Either you apologise, or we stop here.
:::

## Verbs after neither of

Singular or plural both work: _Neither of them **is** home_ / _Neither of them **are** home._

::: examples
- ==Neither of the letters== arrived on time.
- ==Neither of the twins== speaks French.
- ==Neither of these seats== is free.
:::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? 'Saturday or Sunday?' '___ works for me.'
= Either
? 'Did you like the soup or the salad?' '___ . I hate both.'
= Neither
? We tried two garages; ___ of them could help.
= neither
? She speaks ___ German ___ Swedish.
= both / and
? Can ___ of you drive a van?
= either
:::

::: summary
- **both** = the two together; **neither** = not one; **either** = one or the other.
- **of** is optional after both, needed after neither and either before a group.
- Join with **both … and**, **neither … nor**, **either … or**.
- Two things → both/neither/either; more than two → all/none/any.
:::
