## try — the attempt, or the experiment
**try** is one of the most useful verbs to get right, because the two forms answer different questions.

::: form
# 🔀 try + to / -ing
try **to do** | an attempt — usually it didn't work
try **doing** | an experiment — the verb before it keeps its plain form
:::

::: cards
# 🎯 an attempt
_I tried **to reach** the shelf, but I wasn't tall enough._
We ==tried to put out== the fire and failed.
_I'm trying **to concentrate**._ Please stop.
==Try to remember== where you saw her.
# 🧪 an experiment
_Then I tried **knocking** on the window._
Sue ==tried asking== Carl, but he had none.
==Try restarting== it if it freezes.
_He tried **calling** later and got through._
:::

The tell is the **verb before it**. After **try to**, the next verb is plain and the sentence can stand alone. After **try doing**, the sentence only makes sense with that earlier verb:

::: compare
# ✗ tried + to + a verb that needs doing
- ~~I tried **to knocking** on the window.~~
# ✓ tried + doing, because "knock" is the experiment
- I tried **knocking** on the window.
:::

::: note 🧪
_Try **doing** X_ literally means "experiment with doing X". _Try **to do** X_ means "attempt X,
and I'd like this to work." Same verb, two different attitudes — and the one after **try
doing** can never stand on its own.
:::

## need — the duty, or the state of affairs
**need** is similar. **need to** is about what **you** have to do; **need doing** is about what
**the thing** requires.

::: form
# 🔧 two kinds of need
need **to do** | you have a duty
need **doing** | the situation demands it
:::

::: examples
- I ==need to talk== to my landlord. (I have a duty)
- The grass ==needs cutting==. (it has grown too long)
- The screws ==need tightening==. (they are loose)
- You ==need to take== an umbrella. (you should)
- This room ==needs painting==. (the walls are bad)
:::

::: compare
# 🙋 your duty
- You ==need to reply== to the offer.
- The battery ==needs to be replaced==.
# 🔨 the situation
- The windows ==need cleaning==.
- Your brakes ==need checking== before the trip.
:::

## help — both forms, one meaning
**help** is the friendly exception: **to** may be there or not, and nothing changes.

::: examples
- Can you ==help me look for== it? (no *to*)
- Can you ==help me to look for== it? (with *to*)
- I can't help ==laughing== at that. (no *to*)
- We couldn't help ==overhearing== them. (with *to*)
:::

::: note 🤝
With **can't help**, the **-ing** is the only natural shape: _I can't help **smiling**._ In
positive sentences the two forms simply mean the same thing.
:::

::: trap
✗ ~~I tried to knocking on the window.~~
✓ I tried ==knocking== on the window.
✗ ~~Please try to restarting it.~~
✓ ==Try restarting== it.
✗ ~~The grass needs to cutting.~~
✓ The grass ==needs cutting==.
✗ ~~I need cutting the lawn.~~
✓ I ==need to cut== the lawn.
✗ ~~I was worn out. I tried to staying awake, but I couldn't.~~
✓ I was worn out. I ==tried to stay== awake, but I couldn't.
**need to do** = your duty. **need doing** = what the thing requires. Only one of the two fits
each sentence.
:::

::: cards
# 🎯 try to do
_I tried **to fix** it myself._
# 🧪 try doing
_I tried **cooking** without a recipe._
# 🔧 need doing
_The roof **needs repairing**._
# 🙋 need to do
_I **need to call** the plumber._
:::

::: summary
- **try to do** = an attempt; **try doing** = an experiment, and the verb before it stays plain.
- **need to do** = your duty; **need doing** = what the thing requires.
- **help to do** and **help doing** both work and mean the same.
- With **can't help** the **-ing** is the natural shape.
:::
