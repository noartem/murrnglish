hook: 🎬 What's going on right now — and these days?
goals: say what is in progress at this moment · talk about things you're in the middle of · describe changes: getting, rising, starting
---
::: scene 📞 Saturday morning
Leo calls his sister Nina.
> Leo: Hi! Are you busy?
> Nina: A bit. I'm ==painting== the kitchen — there's blue paint everywhere!
> Leo: And where's Max?
> Nina: He's ==helping==. Well… he's ==eating== biscuits and ==watching== me.
:::

## At this very moment
Nina started painting before Leo called, and she hasn't finished. When something is in progress like this — begun but not over — English uses the **present continuous**.

:::: row
::: rule 🧩 How to build it
**am / is / are** + verb**-ing**

Nina ==is painting==. The kids ==are shouting==.
:::
::: timeline
span now-16 now+12 | Nina is painting
point now | Leo calls
:::
::::

::: form ✍️ The three forms
# ➕ Positive
I | am (I'm) | work==ing==
he / she / it | is (she's) | work==ing==
we / you / they | are (we're) | work==ing==
# ➖ Negative
I | 'm not | work==ing==
he / she / it | isn't | work==ing==
we / you / they | aren't | work==ing==
# ❓ Question
am | I | work==ing==?
is | he / she / it | work==ing==?
are | we / you / they | work==ing==?
:::

::: examples
- Shh! The baby ==is sleeping==.
- Why ==are you laughing==? What's so funny?
- Turn the music down, please. ==I'm trying== to concentrate.
- Look outside — ==it's snowing==!
- ==We aren't watching== this. You can switch it off.
:::

:::: row
::: form ✍️ Spelling the -ing form
# e drops off
make | making
write | writing
# one short vowel: double it
sit | sitting
run | running
# ie becomes y
lie | lying
die | dying
:::
::: trap
✗ I ~~reading~~ now.
✓ I'm reading now.
✗ Look! It ~~snows~~.
✓ Look! It's snowing.
You need both parts: _am/is/are_ and the _-ing_ form.
:::
::::
The action doesn't have to be happening at the exact moment you speak. It's enough that it has started and isn't over yet — you're somewhere in the middle of it.

::: scene 🍕 At the pizza place
> Oscar: I'm ==reading== a brilliant novel at the moment. It's about a lighthouse keeper.
Oscar isn't reading as he says this — he's eating pizza. But he's halfway through the book, so it's "in progress" for him.
:::

::: compare
# ⏱️ Right this second
- Hold on, I'm putting my shoes on.
- Who are you texting?
# 📅 These days
- My brother is learning to drive.
- We're renovating our flat, so we're living with my parents.
- Are you still working on that project?
:::

::: words 🗓️ Time words that include now
right now · at the moment · today · this week · this month · this year · these days · currently
:::

::: note
A time word like _this week_ or _these days_ marks a stretch of time that is still going on: ==I'm drinking== far too much coffee this week.
:::

## Things that are changing
The present continuous is also how English talks about situations that are developing — changing a little at a time.

::: cards
# 📈 getting + adjective
Your English ==is getting== better every week.
# 🌡️ rising, falling
Prices ==are rising== again.
# 🌧️ starting to…
It's ==starting== to rain. Let's go inside.
# 😴 how you feel
I'm ==getting== tired. Can we stop soon?
:::

::: timeline
arrow now-30 now+14 | it's getting darker and darker
:::

::: quiz
? Listen! Somebody ___ (knock) at the door.
= is knocking
? ___ (you / enjoy) the film?
= Are you enjoying
? Hurry up — your coffee ___ (get) cold.
= is getting
? Sorry, I can't talk now. I ___ (drive).
= 'm driving
:::

::: summary
- **am / is / are + -ing**: I'm working, she isn't working, are they working?
- For something in progress now: it has started and hasn't finished.
- Also for things around now — _this week_, _these days_ — even if not at this second.
- And for changes: getting better, rising, starting to rain.
:::
