hook: ⏱️ "I burnt myself as I was taking the pan out." One short word, two quite different meanings.
goals: use as for two things happening together · use as because · use just as for the nearest moment · know when to use when instead
---

::: scene 🍳 The kitchen
Three sentences, and **as** doing three different jobs.
I burnt myself ==as I was taking== the pan out. (at the same time)
==As I was hungry==, I ordered an extra sandwich. (because)
==Just as I sat down==, the doorbell went. (at that exact moment)
:::

## as = at the same time

Join two things that happen together. The order is **short clause first**, and you may leave the second **as** out if it is clear.

::: form
# 🕐 together
I burnt myself ==as I was taking== the pan out.
The dog barked ==as the postman came== up the path.

# 🕐 with the second as left out
We all waved goodbye ==(as)== the bus pulled away.
:::

::: note ➖
The two halves describe two actions at the same moment, so the tenses usually match: _was taking_ / _burned_ is fine in speech, and present / past reads cleanly.

:::

## as = because

Put **as** at the front of the first clause and the sentence means _because_.

::: compare
# 🕐 at the same time
- The cyclist wobbled ==as he took the corner==.

# 💡 because
- ==As we don't use the car much==, we've decided to sell it.
- ==As there was nothing in the fridge==, we went out to eat.
:::

## just as

**just as** narrows it to the nearest moment — the two things landed together, almost on top of each other.

::: examples
- ==Just as I sat down==, the doorbell went.
- ==Just as I took the picture==, somebody walked in front of the lens.
- It began to rain ==just as we walked out== of the stadium.
:::

## when, not as

**as** joins two equal actions. **when** marks the moment something else begins. If the two actions are not simultaneous, use **when**.

::: trap
✗ Nils moved abroad as he was twenty.
✓ Nils moved abroad ==when== he was twenty.
✗ I lost my phone as I was travelling.
✓ I lost my phone ==when== I was travelling.
✗ We can walk to the hotel as the streets are quiet.
✓ ==As== the streets are quiet, we can walk to the hotel.
:::

A quick test: ask whether the two actions happen **together**. If not, it is **when**.
## Quiz

::: quiz
? I listened ___ she read her story aloud.
= as
? ___ today is a holiday, the banks are shut.
= As
? The cyclist wobbled ___ he took the corner.
= as
? I lost my passport ___ I was travelling abroad.
= when
? The phone rang ___ I was locking the door.
= just as
? ___ the shops were shut, nobody could buy anything.
= As
? The train slowed down ___ it reached the station.
= as
? I used to swim a lot ___ I was at school.
= when
:::

::: summary
- **as** = at the same time: _I listened as she read her story aloud_.
- **as** at the front = because: _As I was hungry, I ate_.
- **just as** = the nearest moment: the phone went just as I put the kettle on.
- If the actions are not simultaneous, use **when**, not **as**.
:::
