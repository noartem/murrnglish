::: scene 📋 The same facts, three years on
A man describes what has changed — and what has not.
> Rosa: He travels a lot. He travels a lot **still**.
> Hal: He worked in a shop. He does not work there **any more**.
> Rosa: He wrote poems. He **no longer** writes poems.
:::

## still — it has not changed

**still** says: this was true before and it is true now. It goes in the same place as an ordinary adverb.

::: compare
# 🟢 still
- He ==still travels== a lot.
- She is ==still single==.
- I've ==still got== your book.
- Anton lost his job in the spring and he is ==still== out of work.

# 🟢 still, for emphasis
- ==Still== he does not answer his phone.
- ==Still== nobody has written to me.
:::

## any more and no longer

Both say: it was true before and it is not true now. **any more** goes at the end with a present simple or present perfect; **no longer** goes before the main verb.

:::: row
::: compare
# 🔁 stopped, still present tense
- He does not go fishing ==any more==.
- He does not have a beard ==any more==.

# 🔁 stopped, with no longer
- He ==no longer goes== fishing.
- He ==no longer has== a beard.
- He ==no longer works== in a shop.
:::

::: note ↔️
**no longer** is a little more formal, and it can sit before or after the verb: _He **no longer works** here_ / _He **works** here **no longer**._ With **any more** you have no choice — it goes at the end.

:::
::::

## yet — up to now, negative or question

**yet** goes at the end of a negative or a question, and it asks _has it happened by now?_

::: compare
# 🙅 negative
- He hasn't arrived ==yet==.
- She hasn't found a job ==yet==.
- I haven't decided ==yet==.

# ❓ question
- Is dinner ready ==yet==?
- Have you eaten ==yet==?
:::

## already — it has happened

**already** says: this is done, sooner than expected. It normally sits just before the verb or just after **be / have / will**.

::: compare
# ✅ already — it is done
- We have ==already met==.
- She has ==already gone== home.
- Have you had lunch, or shall I put the pan on?

# 🔄 still — it is not done
- We haven't ==met== yet.
- She hasn't ==gone== home yet.
:::

::: trap
✗ He still doesn't work in a shop any more.
✓ He ==no longer works== in a shop.
✗ I have seen already two films tonight.
✓ I have ==already seen== two films tonight.
✗ Have you finished yet dinner?
✓ Have you finished ==dinner yet==?
:::

## From still to yet

A **still**-sentence about something in progress becomes a **not … yet**-sentence with the same meaning.

::: form
# 🔄 the same fact, two ways
It is ==still raining==. → It hasn't ==stopped raining yet==.
He is ==still here==. → He hasn't ==gone yet==.
They are ==still asleep==. → They haven't ==woken up yet==.
:::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? Anton lost his job in the spring and he is ___ out of work.
= still
? Shall I tell Joe, or does he ___ know?
= already
? Are you hungry? Is dinner ready ___ ?
= yet
? I was starving at eight, but now I couldn't eat a thing ___ .
= any more
? She ___ works in Cardiff.
= no longer
? They ___ haven't arrived.
= still
? We've ___ met; there's no need to introduce us.
= already
? Femi said he'd be here at eight. It's half past and he ___ isn't here.
= still
? He has ___ finished his tea.
= already
? We used to be close, but we ___ see each other now.
= hardly ever
:::

::: summary
- **still** = it was true and it still is.
- **any more** at the end, **no longer** before the verb: both mean it has stopped.
- **yet** at the end of a negative or question; **already** near the verb.
- Still + a progressive verb = not … yet: _still asleep_ = _hasn't woken up yet_.
:::
