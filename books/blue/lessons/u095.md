::: scene 🪑 The visitor
Two people are talking about a room, and one of them keeps adding details the other does not need.
We took the small room on the top floor.
We took the small room, which is on the top floor, for four nights.
The window, which faces the yard, sticks.
:::

## A clause in the middle, between commas

A clause that only **adds** information is not part of the main sentence. Wrap it in a pair of commas and it can sit anywhere: at the start, in the middle, at the end.

::: form
# 🔚 at the end
We reached the coast, ==which took five hours by train==.

# 🏁 at the start
==Which took five hours by train==, we reached the coast.

# ↔️ in the middle
We reached the coast, ==which took five hours by train==, and then drove to the sea.
:::

Both commas matter. One of them means the clause is part of the sentence, and then **which** would be the wrong word.

::: note 🔎
A comma on only one side makes an *identifying* clause (unit 92). Commas on **both** sides make an *extra* clause: your listener does not need it to know which coast you mean.
:::

## Which words can do it

::: compare
# 👤 people
- Kate's husband, ==who is a pilot==, flies every week.
- My teacher, ==whose name I have forgotten==, was very kind.

# 📦 things
- We drove to the airport, ==which was not far from the city==.
- We enjoyed the museum, ==where we saw a lot of interesting things==.
:::

The clause can also stand for a whole fact, not a noun: _She passed the exam, **which surprised everybody**._

## Where it cannot go

::: trap
✗ My brother, in which he lives in Cardiff, is a plumber.
✓ My brother, ==who lives in Cardiff==, is a plumber.
✗ She was late, in spite of the bus was late.
✓ She was late, ==although the bus was late==.
✗ The man, who broke his arm, he came to work on Monday.
✓ The man, ==who broke his arm==, came to work on Monday.
:::

An extra clause is never the subject of its sentence, and it never follows a preposition in this style — that is the next unit.

## Quiz

::: quiz
? Nadia is an architect. She works in Manchester.
= Nadia, who works in Manchester, is an architect.
? Omar and Ruth have a son. He is four years old.
= They have a son, who is four years old.
? My flat is on the ground floor. It has a small garden.
= My flat, which has a small garden, is on the ground floor.
? We drove to the coast. We got there after dark.
= We drove to the coast, where we got there after dark.
? Sara is away a lot. Her work takes her abroad.
= Sara, whose work takes her abroad, is away a lot.
? The bus was late. We missed the first half of the match.
= The bus was late, which meant we missed the first half of the match.
:::
::: summary
- An extra clause adds a detail the listener does not need: it goes between **two commas**.
- **who**, **which**, **whose** and **where** all work; so does a clause standing for a whole fact.
- With one comma it is an identifying clause and needs **who / that / which** (unit 92).
- No preposition before **which**, and never **in spite of** here — use **although**.
:::
