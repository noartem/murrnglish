::: scene 🎂 The surprise that wasn't
Last Friday Lina got home at seven, ready for a quiet evening.
The lights ==were== off, but someone ==had left== balloons in the hall.
Her friends ==had planned== a surprise party — and her little brother ==had== already ==told== her about it.
:::

## Had + past participle
The **past perfect** is **had** + past participle, the same for every person. You use it when you're already talking about the past and need to go back to an **earlier** past.

:::: row
::: form
# ➕ / ➖
I / you / he / she / we / they | ==had== / ==hadn't== | left
# ❓
==had== | I / you / she… | left?
# ✂️
I'd · she'd · they'd = I had…
:::
::: timeline
point 25 | brother told her
point 55 | Lina got home
point now | now
:::
::::

::: rule ⏪
**Past moment** → past simple: _Lina got home._

**Before that moment** → past perfect: _Her brother ==had told== her._
:::

## Already, just, never … before
These words often travel with the past perfect.

::: cards
# ⏰ already
When we got to the station, the train ==had already left==.
# ⚡ just
We weren't hungry. We=='d just had== pizza.
# 🆕 never … before
Hugo was nervous. He=='d never sung== in public ==before==.
:::

::: examples
- I didn't recognise my old school. It ==had changed== completely.
- Pia couldn't come to dinner. She=='d made== other plans.
- It was great to see Tariq. I ==hadn't seen== him for years.
:::

## Had done or did?
If you tell events **in order**, the past simple is enough. Use the past perfect when the story jumps **back** to something earlier.

::: compare
# ➡️ in order: did, did
- Sara ==left==. Then I ==arrived==.
- When I arrived, Sara ==left==. (she left after I came)
# ⏪ jumping back: had done
- I arrived. Sara ==had left==.
- When I arrived, Sara ==had left==. (she was already gone)
:::

::: trap
✗ Sorry I'm late. The bus ~~had broken~~ down on the way.
✓ Sorry I'm late. The bus broke down on the way.
:::

::: note 🧭
The past perfect needs a past moment to look back from. If there's no other past in the story, use the past simple.
:::

::: quiz
? The flat was very clean when I got back. Somebody ___ (tidy) it.
= had tidied
? I was tired, so I ___ (go) to bed early.
= went
? When we reached the cinema, the film ___ (already / start).
= had already started
? Zara ___ (never / ride) a camel before her trip to Morocco.
= had never ridden / 'd never ridden
:::

::: summary
- **had + past participle** = an earlier past.
- It looks back from a past moment told in the past simple.
- **already, just, never … before** often go with it.
- Events told in order → past simple is enough.
:::
