## even = more than you expected

**even** points at the person or thing that breaks the pattern. The others did it, so why mention this one?

:::: row
::: compare
# 🟢 even
- Kate doesn't like hotels, but she liked this one, ==even Kate==.
- The film was long. ==Even the director== fell asleep.
- It's a small shop, and it sells ==even bread==.

# 🔄 not even — nothing did
- Nobody enjoyed the gallery. ==Not even Amy==, who loves art.
- None of them took a picture. ==Not even Lisa==, the photographer.
:::

::: note 📌
**even** is not a polite way of saying _also_. _Even Kate liked it_ does not mean _Kate also liked it_; it means _you would not have expected Kate to like it._

:::
::::

## even + comparative

With a comparative, **even** says the second one is more extreme than the first.

::: examples
- It was very hot yesterday, so today it's ==even hotter==.
- The church is 700 years old; the house next door is ==even older==.
- I did badly in the exam, and most of my friends did ==even worse==.
- Neither of us was hungry; I left most of it and he left ==even more==.
:::

## Negative + even

Put **even** after **don't / can't / didn't / won't** to make the negative stronger.

::: compare
# 🔧 the negative, then even
- She didn't ==even say hello==.
- I can't ==even remember her name==.
- There isn't ==even a cinema== in this town.
- He didn't ==even text== his wife from the airport.

# 🔧 the everyday order
- She didn't say ==even hello==.
:::

## even if and even though

Two clauses of **if** and **even if** talk about a possibility. **though** and **even though** talk about something you know to be true.

::: compare
# ❓ possible, not yet decided
- ==If== you come, I'll come too.
- ==Even if== you come, I'll come too.
- You can do ==as you like==.

# ✅ known to be true
- ==Even though== he has never flown, he isn't frightened.
- ==Even though== I hadn't eaten for a day, I wasn't hungry.
:::

::: trap
✗ Nobody enjoys it, even Kate.
✓ Nobody enjoys it, ==not even Kate==.
✗ Even with the heating on, the room was cold.
✓ ==Even with== the heating on, the room was cold.
✗ Even if she is nervous, she speaks very clearly.
✓ ==Even though== she is nervous, she speaks very clearly.
:::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? Nobody wanted the soup. ___ Lisa, who eats everything.
= Not even
? It was cold yesterday, but today it's ___ .
= even colder
? He didn't ___ his phone with him.
= even bring
? She can ___ hear the music downstairs.
= even
? The train is late, ___ we catch the next one.
= even if
? ___ she is nervous, she speaks very clearly.
= Even though
? The queue was short, ___ on a Saturday morning.
= even
? Nobody rang, ___ our old neighbour.
= not even
:::

::: summary
- **even** marks the person or thing that breaks the pattern: _even Kate_.
- **not even** makes a negative stronger: _not even Amy_, _didn't even call_.
- With a comparative, **even** pushes it further: _even hotter_, _even less_.
- **if / even if** = possible; **though / even though** = already true.
:::
