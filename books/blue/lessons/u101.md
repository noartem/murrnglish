## good or well

**good** is an adjective, so it goes before a noun or after a linking verb. **well** is the adverb.

::: compare
# 🏷️ good
- Joe's exam results were very ==good==.
- She looks ==good== on that coat.
- It is a ==good== idea to leave early.

# 🏃 well
- Joe did ==well== in his exams.
- I can't hear you very ==well==.
:::

After a linking verb, English normally prefers **good**: the soup tastes ==good==, not _well_ — unless you mean _well_ as in _in good health_.

::: note 📌
**well** can also be an adjective meaning _healthy_: she has been ill, and she isn't well.
:::

## Words built with well

A few words are made by putting **well-** in front of a past participle, with a hyphen.

::: words
well-behaved · well-built · well-done · well-informed · well-known · well-made
well-paid · well-kept · well-written
:::

Each one is really two short sentences joined: _She is well paid_ → _she is paid well_ → **well-paid**.

::: examples
- a ==well-known== singer
- a ==well-written== novel
- a ==well-kept== garden
- a ==well-informed== journalist
:::

## hard and hardly

::: trap
✗ I tried hardly to remember her name.
✓ I tried ==hard== to remember her name.
✗ This coat is practically unused. I have hard worn it.
✓ I have ==hardly== worn it.
:::

**hard** = a lot, with effort. **hardly** = almost not.

::: compare
# 🏋️ hard
- She trains ==hard== every morning.
- Please push ==harder==.
- The question was ==hard==.

# 🪶 hardly
- I can ==hardly== hear you.
- There was ==hardly anyone== at the door.
- He has ==hardly changed== in twenty years.
:::

The same happens with **late** (not on time) and **lately** (recently).

::: compare
# ⏰ late = not on time
- The train arrived ==late==.
- We got up ==late== again.

# 🗓️ lately = recently
- I haven't seen him ==lately==.
- Have you been to the gym ==lately==?
:::

## hardly + the negative words

After **hardly**, English goes straight to the plain word: any, anybody, anything, anywhere, ever — and **hardly ever** for "almost never".

::: form
# 📉 hardly + …
There was ==hardly any== wind. (= almost no wind)
There was ==hardly anybody== staying there. (= almost nobody)
There is ==hardly anything== to eat. (= almost nothing)
There was ==hardly anywhere== to sit. (= almost nowhere)
I ==hardly ever== watch television.
:::

::: note ⚠️
Not _hardly no_, not _hardly nothing_. **hardly** already means _almost not_, so it never takes _no_ or _nothing_.
:::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? Her English is very ___.
= good
? She speaks English very ___.
= well
? This is a ___-paid job.
= well
? I ___ slept last night; I feel odd.
= hardly
? He trains ___ every morning.
= hard
? The hotel was empty. There was ___ at the desk.
= hardly anyone
? We used to be close, but we ___ see each other now.
= hardly ever
? I haven't been to the cinema ___.
= lately
? Please push ___ — the door is stuck.
= hard
? She is a ___-known writer.
= well
:::

::: summary
- **good** before a noun or after a linking verb; **well** with an action.
- **well-** + past participle makes a new word: well-known, well-paid, well-written.
- **hard** = with effort; **hardly** = almost not. **late** = not on time; **lately** = recently.
- After **hardly**: any, anybody, anything, anywhere, ever — never no or nothing.
:::
