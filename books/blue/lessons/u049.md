::: scene 🛋️ The neighbour from number 30
Marta has just moved into number 12. Across the road a man is watering his plants. She takes a chance and calls out.
> Marta: Excuse me! ==Are you== the man from number 30?
> Hal: I am. Hal.
> Marta: ==Were you born here==, or did you move?
> Hal: Oh, I grew up in Leeds. ==When did you== come?
> Marta: Last spring. ==What do you do== these days?
> Hal: I restore old clocks. ==Your hands are== very steady!
> Marta: ==Don't you ever get tired== of fixing things at night?
:::

## The subject moves after the verb
An English question usually puts the **subject after the first verb**. The tense of the verb stays exactly what it would be in a statement.

::: form
# 🔄 statement → question
Kalle will arrive | ==will Kalle arrive?==
you have finished | ==have you finished?==
the shop was open | ==was the shop open?==
:::

That's why _Is Sonya working?_ is right and _Is working Sonya_ is not: **is** already carries the tense, so nothing else moves.

::: note 🔁
With the verb _be_ the question is just **be + subject** — _Are you ready?_ For every other verb an auxiliary comes first and the subject follows it.
:::

## Do, does, did
Questions about habits, facts and the past need an **auxiliary**. Add **do**, **does** or **did**, put the subject after it, and go back to the **plain verb** at the end.

::: rule 🧩 three moves
1. Choose **do** / **does** / **did**.
2. Slot the subject in right after it.
3. Finish with the verb's plain form.

_Rafa texted a friend_ → _Did **Rafa text** a friend?_
:::

::: examples
- you ==live== nearby? → ==do you live== nearby?
- the lesson ==starts== at nine? → ==does the lesson start== at nine?
- you ==sold== your car? → ==did you sell== your car?
- the bus ==stopped== early? → ==did the bus stop== early?
:::

::: note ⏱️
**did** carries the past tense by itself, so the main verb never adds **-ed**: _Did you **saw** it?_ is wrong — _Did you **see** it?_ is right.
:::

## When who or what is the subject, no do
When **who** or **what** is *itself* the subject, there is no **do** at all. The question word goes straight to the front.

::: compare
# ✓ the question word is the doer
- ==Who== paid the bill?
- ==What== happened to you last night?
- ==How many== people came?
- ==Which bus== goes to the centre?
# ✗ do added by mistake
- ~~Who did pay the bill?~~
- ~~What did happen to you?~~
- ~~How many did come?~~
- ~~Which bus does go to the centre?~~
:::

The test: ask yourself **is somebody or something doing it?** If yes, no _do_.

::: note 🪞
The same person can be the doer or the one affected, and the word order flips:

_Nadia emailed **Marek**_ → _Who did Nadia email?_
_**Marek** emailed Nadia_ → _Who emailed Nadia?_
:::

## Prepositions go at the end
With **who**, **what**, **which** and **where**, the preposition usually comes last.

::: examples
- Where ==are you from==?
- ==What are you waiting for==?
- ==Which job did she apply for==?
:::

A formal alternative puts the preposition first and adds **whom**: _To whom do you wish to speak?_ In conversation, leave it at the end.

## Don't you…? — surprise, or a gentle check
A **negative question** says you expected the opposite, or it asks for confirmation of something you half know.

::: cards
# 😲 real surprise
"Didn't you hear the bell? I rang it three times."
# 🤝 checking what you think
"Haven't we met before?" "Yes, I think we have."
# 😕 asking after somebody
"Don't you like him? He seems nice."
# 🍽️ making a suggestion
"Why don't we eat out tonight?"
# 😕 asking about yesterday
"Why wasn't Devi at work yesterday?"
:::

::: note ⚠️ yes and no flip
_“Don't you want to go?”_
**Yes** = yes, I want to go.
**No** = no, I don't.

Your listener answers what they want, not the words _don't you_.
:::

::: trap
✗ ~~Don't you like him?~~
✓ Don't you ==like== him?
✗ ~~Why you didn't phone me?~~
✓ Why ==didn't you phone== me?
✗ ~~Why you are angry?~~
✓ Why ==are you== angry?
The auxiliary jumps in front of **you**, and the main verb drops back to its plain form.
:::

::: quiz
? You ___ to work by bike? (every day)
= do you go / Do you go
? ___ the film start at eight? (what time / the)
= what time does
? ___ paid the bill? (who)
= who
? ___ you like the new restaurant?
= don't
? Why ___ you tell me earlier? (not / say)
= didn't you say
? ___ your parents live in Leeds? (where / do)
= where do / Where do
:::

::: summary
- Questions put the **subject after the first verb**.
- **do / does / did** in the present and past, then the **plain verb**.
- No **do** when **who / what / which / how many** is the subject.
- Prepositions at the **end**: _Who are you waiting for?_
- **Don't you…? / Why don't you…?** for surprise, checking and suggestions.
- After **why** the question goes negative: _Why didn't you tell me?_
:::
