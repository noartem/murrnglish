::: scene 🚪 A colleague who keeps the window open
Sanne has been working next to Ravi all summer. The window is wide open. The air conditioner is on.
> Sanne: Shall I shut the window, or ==would you like me to do== it?
> Ravi: Please. ==I'm going to ask her to shut== it.
> Sanne: You always ask her to do things.
> Ravi: She ==reminded me to close== it yesterday, and ==warned me to be careful== with the latch.
> Sanne: I ==advised you to open== it. You never listen.
> Ravi: It's her flat. She ==let me keep== it open.
:::

## Somebody has to do it
To ask somebody to act, put a **person** in the middle: **verb + person + to + verb**.

::: rule 🧩 the frame
verb + **person** + **to** + verb

_She warned **him** to be careful._
_They invited **him** to stay._

_who should do it_ · _what they should do_
:::

::: words
allow · advise · ask · convince · encourage · force · invite · order · persuade · remind · teach · tell · warn · want
:::

::: examples
- They ==invited her to stay== with them.
- He ==reminded me to lock== the door.
- She ==warned us to be careful== on the ice.
- I ==asked him to carry== the box.
- The teacher ==encouraged them to try== again.
- My lawyer ==advised me not to sign== anything.
:::

The negative is **not to**: _She warned us **not to touch** the box._

## The two polite offers
When you are the one offering to act, there are two fixed shapes and both need the **person** — _me_ — in the middle.

::: form
# 🙋 offering to do something
Do you want me to …? | ==Do you want me to shut== the window?
Would you like me to …? | ==Would you like me to shut== it?
Would you like me to …? | ==Would you like me to lend== you some?
:::

::: note 🔁
Both mean the same thing. **Do you want me to…?** is plainer; **Would you like me to…?** is a
little more formal. Neither needs an answer longer than _please_ or _no, thanks_.
:::

## Rewriting the same idea
Many pairs of sentences mean the same thing, and the second one uses **to**:

::: form
# 🔄 plain → with to
He said I could use his car. | His father ==allowed me to use== his car.
I was surprised it rained. | I ==didn't expect it to rain==.
She made him work harder. | She ==made him work== harder.
A car lets you get about. | A car ==enables you to get== about.
:::

::: examples
- Tom looks older in glasses. → Tom's glasses ==make him look== older.
- I think you should know. → I ==want you to know== the truth.
- Sarah talked me into it. → Sarah ==persuaded me to apply==.
- I was warned about him. → I ==was warned not to believe== everything he says.
:::

## Three verbs that drop the to
**make**, **let** and **help** are the famous three. With **make** and **let** the infinitive has **no to**.

::: compare
# ✗ to after make / let
- ~~It made me **to cry**.~~
- ~~Let me **to finish**.~~
- ~~Sarah won't let me **to drive** her car.~~
# ✓ no to
- It made me ==cry==.
- Let me ==finish==.
- Sarah won't let me ==drive== her car.
:::

**Help** is the flexible one — both forms are correct and mean the same:

::: note 🤝
_I can't help **smiling**_ = _I can't help **to smile**._ _She helped me **pack**_ = _She helped
me **to pack**._ Choose whichever flows; both are standard.
:::

::: trap
✗ ~~She warned to be careful.~~
✓ She warned ==me to be careful==.
✗ ~~They invited to stay.~~
✓ They invited ==him to stay==.
✗ ~~You are not allowed take photos here.~~
✓ You are not allowed ==to take== photos here.
✗ ~~What do you advise me do?~~
✓ What do you advise me ==to do==?
✗ ~~You can't make people to do things.~~
✓ You can't make people ==do== things.
The **person** is required after these verbs — except in **I don't want you to…**, where the
subject *you* is the person. And **make**/**let** never take *to*.
:::

::: cards
# 🔔 invite
"Come to my party." — She ==invited me to her== party.
# ⚠️ warn
"Mind the step." — He ==warned us to mind== the step.
# 💬 advise
"Don't sign it." — My lawyer ==advised me not to sign== it.
# 🕊️ remind
"Don't forget." — She ==reminded me to lock== the door.
# 🙋 offer
"Close it?" — ==Would you like me to close== it?
:::

::: summary
- Ask somebody to act: **verb + person + to + verb** — warned him to…, invited them to….
- Two offers: **Do you want me to…?** and **Would you like me to…?**
- Many pairs of sentences rewrite with **to**: _She persuaded me to apply._
- **make**, **let** and **help** drop the **to**.
- The negative is **not to**.
:::
