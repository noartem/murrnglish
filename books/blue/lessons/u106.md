## How much better?

A word in front of the comparative says **by how much**. These are the ones you will meet most.

::: form
# 📏 strength
a bit / slightly + comparative
a lot / **much** / far / considerably + comparative

# 📏 examples
a bit cooler · a little slower
much bigger · a lot more interesting
far more complicated · considerably higher
:::

::: note 🎯
**much, a lot, far** go with **more + adjective**, never with **-er**: _much bigger_, not _much biger_. With **-er** words like _good_ and _bad_ they work fine: _much better._

:::

## any and no

**any** in a negative means _at all_, however little. **no** says _not more than_, and it is followed by **than**.

::: compare
# 🙅 negative + any
- I couldn't get here ==any sooner==.
- I can't walk ==any further==.
- The shop isn't expensive; the prices are ==no higher than== anywhere else.

# 🙅 negative + no
- The traffic isn't bad today; it's ==no worse than== usual.
- Prices here are ==no higher than== in the city.
:::

## more and more

Put the comparative **twice**, joined by **and**, to show a continuing change.

::: examples
- The hole is getting ==bigger and bigger==.
- I waited and became ==more and more nervous==.
- Her English has got ==better and better==.
- Prices are going ==higher and higher==.
- These days I am ==more and more tired==.
:::

## The … the …

Two halves, each of them a clause, each with its own **the**. The meaning is "as X goes up, so does Y".

:::: row
::: compare
# 🔁 the … the …
- The ==sleeper== you are, the ==harder it is to get up==.
- The ==sooner== we start, the ==better==.
- The ==more I know==, the ==less I understand==.

# ⬇️ with opposite halves
- The ==more== he explained, the ==more impatient== she became.
:::

::: trap
✗ The more tired you get, harder it is to work.
✓ The more tired you get, the ==harder== it is to work.
✗ I need a thing more big.
✓ I need something ==much bigger==.
✗ It's not worse than usual.
✓ It's ==no worse== than usual.
:::
::::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? The problem is ___ than we thought.
= much more serious
? Today is ___ cooler than yesterday.
= a little
? I need something ___ ; this bag is too small.
= much bigger
? I can't get up ___ than eight.
= any later
? The traffic isn't bad today — it's ___ than usual.
= no worse
? That hole is getting ___ and ___ .
= bigger / bigger
? The ___ I explain, the ___ he looks.
= more / confused
? You look ___ today than you did on Monday.
= slightly older
? I walked ___, so I was the last to arrive.
= more slowly
:::

::: summary
- **much / a lot / far / a bit / slightly** say how big the difference is.
- Negative + **any** = _at all_; **no** + comparative = _not more than_, and takes **than**.
- **comparative and comparative** = a continuing change: bigger and bigger.
- **The … the …** joins two halves, each with its own **the**.
:::
