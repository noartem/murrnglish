## Have and have got
For what you **own**, your **family**, how things **look** and **illness**, you can say **have** or **have got**. They mean the same; _have got_ is common in British speech.

::: cards
# 🚲 owning
Oscar ==has got== two bikes. / Oscar ==has== two bikes.
# 👨‍👩‍👧 family
I=='ve got== a twin brother.
# 💇 looks
Nell ==has== curly hair.
# 🤒 illness
I=='ve got== a cold. / I ==have== a cold.
:::

::: form
# 🔹 have
I ==don't have== a car.
==Do== you ==have== a pen?
# 🔸 have got
I ==haven't got== a car.
==Have== you ==got== a pen?
:::

::: trap
✗ I'm ~~having~~ a headache.
✓ I've got a headache. / I have a headache.
✗ How many cousins ~~have you~~?
✓ How many cousins do you have? / have you got?
:::

## In the past: had, didn't have
In the past there's no _got_ and no _hadn't_ on its own: use **had / didn't have / did you have**.

:::: row
::: examples
- When I was ten, I ==had== a hamster.
- We ==didn't have== a map, so we got lost.
- ==Did== you ==have== a good time?
:::
::: trap
✗ I ~~hadn't~~ any money.
✓ I didn't have any money.
✗ Lisa ~~had got~~ a dog as a child.
✓ Lisa had a dog as a child.
:::
::::

## Have for actions
**Have** also means _do_ or _take_ in many expressions. Then it's an action, so it works like any verb: **do you have…?**, **I'm having…**. You can't use _have got_ here.

::: words 🍽️ have + …
have breakfast · have lunch · have a shower · have a break · have a look · have a chat · have a party · have a baby · have a nice time · have trouble (doing) · have a holiday
:::

::: compare
# 🧱 have = own (state)
- I ==have== a bath at home. (there's one)
- Max ==had== a beard when I met him.
# 🏃 have = an action
- I=='m having== a bath. Call back later.
- How often ==do you have== a haircut?
:::

::: quiz
? We ___ (not / have) a garden, so we grow herbs on the balcony.
= don't have / haven't got
? I couldn't phone you. I ___ (not / have) my phone with me.
= didn't have
? Sorry, Dad can't talk now. He ___ a shower.
= 's having / is having
? ___ you ___ a nice time at the party last night?
= Did … have
:::

::: summary
- **have / have got** = own, family, looks, illness (not continuous).
- Questions and negatives: _do you have / don't have_ or _have you got / haven't got_.
- Past: **had, didn't have, did you have** (no _got_).
- **have** for actions (have lunch, have a break) can be continuous; no _got_.
:::
