## at, on, in

The choice follows the size of the unit: a **moment** takes **at**, a **day** takes **on**, a **longer period** takes **in**.

::: form
# 🕐 at — a time of day
at five o'clock
at midday
at sunset
They arrived ==at 5 o'clock==.
# 📅 on — a day or a date
on Friday
on 16 May 2012
on my birthday
They arrived ==on Friday==.
# 🗓️ in — a month, a year, a season
in June
in the 1990s
in winter
They arrived ==in June==.
:::

The three stack, from small to large: _at five o'clock **on** Friday **in** June._

## The fixed phrases

Some combinations are simply fixed. Learn them as single items.

::: compare
# 📌 the usual ones
- ==at the moment== / ==at present== (= now)
- ==at the same time==
- ==at the weekend== (British), ==on the weekend== (American)
- ==at Christmas==, but ==on Christmas Day==
- ==in the morning / afternoon / evening==
# 📌 the two that catch people
- ==at night== for nights in general; ==in the night== for one particular night
- ==on Friday morning==, not ~~in Friday morning~~
:::

## No preposition before next, last, this, every

**next**, **last**, **this** and **every** already fix the time, so **at / on / in** is left out.

::: trap
✗ I'll see you ==on next Friday==.
✓ I'll see you ==next Friday==.
✗ He left school ==in last June==.
✓ He left school ==last June==.
✗ I don't work ==on Monday mornings==.
✓ I don't work ==Monday mornings==.
✗ He was born ==at 10 May 1993==.
✓ He was born ==on 10 May 1993==.
✗ She left ==in last week==.
✓ She left ==last week==.
:::

With plain days **on** is often dropped altogether: _See you Friday._ / _I play tennis Sunday mornings._

## in … = from now, or how long it took

**in** also measures forward from now, or the time a process takes.

::: timeline
span now future | a moment · a week · six months
point now | now
point future+4 | in a moment
point future+30 | in a week
:::

::: examples
- They'll be here ==in a moment==. ==(very soon)==
- He's been away since Monday; he's back ==on Friday==.
- I learnt to drive ==in three months==. ==(it took three months)==
:::

Two meanings hide in _in a week_: _a week from now_ **or** _during that week_. Context decides which.

## Quiz

::: quiz
? Mozart was born ___ Salzburg in 1756.
= in / on
? I have been asked to a wedding ___ 14 February.
= on / in
? Sam is not here ___ the moment.
= at / on
? My flight arrives ___ 5 o'clock ___ the morning.
= at / in
? She retires ___ two years.
= in / for
? The office is shut ___ Wednesday afternoons.
= on / in
? Do you give each other presents ___ Christmas?
= at / on
? A shutter banged ___ the night. What was it?
= at / in
? I will see you ___ Friday.
= on / at
? We waited ___ the bus for forty minutes.
= for / in
? The train leaves ___ five minutes.
= in / at
:::

::: summary
- **at** for a time of day, **on** for a day or date, **in** for a month, a year, a season.
- Fixed: _at the moment, at the same time, at Christmas (on Christmas Day), in the morning (on Friday morning)._
- No **at / on / in** before **next, last, this, every**: _next Friday, last June, every Monday._
- **in** can also mean *from now* or *how long it took*: _in a week, in four weeks._
:::
