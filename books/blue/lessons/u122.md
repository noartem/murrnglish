## on time and in time

**on time** means a thing happens at the moment it was meant to. **in time** means a person is not too late for it.

::: compare
# ✅ on time — punctual, as planned
- The 8.40 service left ==on time==.
- The film started ==on time==, for once.
- She always arrives ==on time==.
# 🏃 in time — not late
- We reached the station ==in time== to catch the connection.
- I filed the report ==in time== for the deadline.
- He arrived ==just in time==.
:::

Change one and the meaning flips: _The bus was **on time**, but we arrived **in time** for nothing._

::: trap
✗ The film didn't begin ==in time==, so we missed the opening scene.
✓ The film didn't begin ==on time==, so we missed the opening scene.
✗ We nearly missed it. We got to the station ==on time==.
✓ We nearly missed it. We got to the station ==in time==.
✗ The trains are rarely ==in time== in this country.
✓ The trains are rarely ==on time== in this country.
✗ He apologised ==in time== for keeping me waiting.
✓ He apologised ==on time== for keeping me waiting. ==(at once)==
:::

## just in time

**just in time** is **in time** with **just** in front: the moment came very close.

::: examples
- I managed to brake ==just in time==.
- The class began ==just in time== to catch the last bus.
- She shut the tap ==just in time==.
:::

## at the end + a noun

**at the end** with **of** and a noun names the last point of an event, a period or a process.

::: form
# 🏁 at the end of + a noun
at the end of the game
at the end of the month
at the end of the interview
at the end of the course
at the end of the race
:::

Without **of**, **at the end** usually means *at that moment*: _At the end, everyone clapped._

## in the end + a clause

**in the end** means *after all the changes and delays, the outcome was…*. It often takes a full clause.

::: examples
- ==In the end== the old house was sold.
- ==In the end== she handed in her notice.
- ==In the end== we decided against the trip.
- ==In the end I== gave up on learning Japanese.
:::

The test: after **in the end** you can often put a whole clause, after **at the end** you normally cannot. _At the end I resigned_ sounds odd; _in the end I resigned_ sounds normal.

## at the end or in the end?

::: compare
# 📍 at the end — the last moment
- The two sides shook hands ==at the end of the match==.
- I get paid ==at the end of the month==.
- ==At the end of the lesson==, everyone filed out.
# 🔚 in the end — the final outcome
- ==In the end== he apologised.
- ==In the end== we had to order a taxi.
- She never went to Japan, and ==in the end== she didn't mind.
:::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? The bus is usually ___ , but it was late this morning.
= on time / in time
? We nearly missed our train; we reached the platform just ___ .
= in time / on time
? It is hoped the new bridge will be finished ___ for the summer.
= in time / on time
? Why are you never ___ ? You keep everybody waiting.
= on time / in time
? A child stepped into the road and you braked ___ .
= just in time / on time
? The two sides shook hands ___ of the match.
= at the end / in the end
? We sat in traffic for hours, and ___ we took a taxi.
= in the end / at the end
? I get paid ___ of every month.
= at the end / in the end
? There were problems at first, but ___ everything was fine.
= in the end / at the end
? ___ of the lecture, the professor took questions.
= At the end / In the end
? Your friend was going to sit on your new chair. You stopped him ___ .
= just in time / on time
? The forms must reach us ___ or the offer lapses.
= in time / on time
? She still hasn't replied ___ three weeks.
= since / for
? I'm tired ___ I finally put the phone down.
= until / by
:::

::: summary
- **on time** = the thing happens when it was meant to; **in time** = the person is not too late.
- **just in time** = in time by a hair: _We got in just in time._
- **at the end of** + a noun = the last point of an event; **at the end** alone = *at that moment*.
- **in the end** = *after all the changes and delays*: _In the end the house was sold._
:::
