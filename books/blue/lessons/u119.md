## for = how long

**for** counts the length of a period, and goes with a length of time: two hours, three days, a week.

::: form
# ⏱ for + a length
for an hour · for two days · for a week · for years
She slept for ten hours.
I haven't seen him for ages.
:::

Compare **in**, which puts a length *inside* a period: _in a week_ can also mean _during the coming week_.

## during = at what point

**during** names the event, the period or the stretch of time *in which* something happens.

::: compare
# 📌 during — a named period
- Production stopped ==during the strike==.
- I read the whole book ==during the flight==.
- We met Sue ==during the interval==.
# ⏳ for — the length itself
- The strike ==lasted for three months==.
- The flight ==lasted for two hours==.
:::

The same story with both: ==The strike lasted for three months, during which production stopped entirely.==

## while = two things together

**while** joins **two clauses** and says they overlap. What follows it is a subject and a verb — never a bare period.

::: examples
- Don't interrupt me ==while I'm speaking==.
- I met Mike ==while I was shopping==.
- She read a book ==while the soup heated==.
- What are you going to do ==while we're in Lisbon==?
:::

**while** needs two actions. With only one, use **during** or **for**.

::: trap
✗ I read the book ==during I was on the train==.
✓ I read the book ==while I was on the train==.
✗ She slept ==for the house was quiet==.
✓ She slept ==while the house was quiet==.
✗ We met ==during we were students==.
✓ We met ==while we were students==.
✗ The shop is open ==during 9 a.m. and 6 p.m.==.
✓ The shop is open ==from 9 a.m. to 6 p.m.==.
:::

## The same story, twice over

::: form
# 📐 the facts
: Maria read a novel for six hours. It was quiet all evening.
# 📌 during
: ==During== the quiet evening, Maria read a novel for six hours.
# ⏳ while
: Maria read a novel ==while== it was quiet all evening.
:::

::: summary
- **for** + a length of time says *how long*: _for six hours, for a week._
- **during** + a period, an event or a stretch of time says *when it happened*: _during the flight, during the storm._
- **while** + a clause joins **two actions that overlap**: _She read while the soup heated._ It never takes a bare period.
- **while** needs a subject and a verb after it; **during** cannot stand in front of one.
:::
