## The -ing clause

Put an **-ing** form straight after the noun and that noun keeps acting. The clause says what the person or thing is doing — usually at the same moment as the verb, or still going on.

::: form
# 👀 doing something
the woman ==talking to the porter==
the path ==leading down to the river==
the man ==asleep beside me on the plane==
a factory ==employing five hundred people==

# 🔨 done to it
the boy ==injured in the accident==
the gate ==damaged in the storm==
the paintings ==stolen from the museum==
our friends' house ==surrounded by trees==
:::

An **-ing** clause leaves the noun busy. An **-ed** clause says the action happened *to* the noun, so the noun just sits there and receives it.

::: note 🧭
The same test works with any tense: it is **-ing** when the noun is the one acting, **-ed** when it is the one being acted on.
:::

## Verbs that take which form

This is not something you can work out from the verb's meaning — the pairs have to be learnt.

::: compare
# ✅ -ing: the noun acts
live · work · sit · study · walk · wait · help · offer · belong · look

# ✅ -ed: the noun is acted on
injure · damage · steal · involve · surprise · frighten · surround · lock
:::

A verb can do both, and then the meaning decides: a picture ==painted by his father== is finished, but a man ==painting a picture== is busy.

## Where the clause sits

::: examples
- I was woken up by an alarm ==going off==.
- Marta showed me some sketches ==drawn by her son==.
- A week after the interview, I received a message ==offering me the job==.
- Ian has a brother ==working== in a bank and a sister ==studying== economics.
- Life must be very hard for people ==living close to busy airports==.
:::

## there is and there was

The same two clauses work after **there is** and **there was**, with no other change.

::: compare
# 🟢 still going on
- There's somebody ==coming==.
- There were a lot of people ==travelling==.
- There was nobody ==staying== there.

# 🔴 already done
- There was nobody ==injured==.
- There was nothing ==written on it==.
- There’s a course ==beginning next Monday==.
:::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? The path ___ to the river is closed.
= leading
? The boy ___ in the crash is in hospital.
= injured
? We repaired the gate ___ in the storm.
= damaged
? The company sent me a ___ with all the details.
= brochure
? There were a lot of people ___ on the platform.
= waiting
? I received an email ___ me the job.
= offering
? The pictures ___ from the museum have not been found.
= stolen
? Ian has a sister ___ economics in Manchester.
= studying
:::

::: summary
- An **-ing** clause after a noun says the noun is doing it: the man ==waiting outside==.
- An **-ed** clause says it was done to the noun: the gate ==damaged in the storm==.
- The verb decides the form; when both work, ask who is acting.
- The clause works after **there is / there was** too: There was nobody ==injured==.
:::
