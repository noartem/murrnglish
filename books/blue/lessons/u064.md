## to + verb — why you did it
After a verb of doing or going, **to + verb** gives the **purpose**: what the action was *for*. It
is shorter than a full _because_ or _in order to_ clause, and it is the ordinary way English
gives a reason.

::: form
# 🎯 purpose
verb + **to** + verb

_I rang the agency **to check** whether it was still listed._
_I moved house **to be** nearer the river._
_She took on a helper **to share** the paperwork._

_the action_ · _what it was for_
:::

::: examples
- I knocked twice ==to wake him up==.
- She left early ==to catch the last train==.
- I keep a notebook ==to write down names==.
- He hired a van ==to move the piano==.
- I left the office early ==to collect my son==.
:::

The **to** here is an **infinitive**, so a plain verb follows it — as in _I rang **to see** if
anyone was home._ Contrast [[u60|looking forward to seeing]], where **to** is a preposition and
**-ing** is compulsory.


## for or to
With a **noun**, use **for**. With a **verb**, use **to**. One rule, and it covers a great deal.

::: form
# 🔀 for + noun · to + verb
an appointment **for** the dentist | an hour **to** fill the form
a queue **for** the tickets | time **to** wait
a reason **to** leave | a present **for** Ana
:::

::: examples
- I made an appointment ==for the dentist==.
- She was late ==for the meeting==.
- We've queued ==for the tickets== for an hour.
- There was a lot of delay ==on the way== here.
- I need a couple of hours ==to finish== this.
- She bought a whistle ==for her nephew==.
:::

::: note 📮
**For** names the thing; **to** names the action. _A bag **for** the shopping_ is a bag;
_a bag **to put** your shopping in_ is what you do with it. The same split runs right through
adjectives too — see [[u65|adjective + to]].
:::

## so that — joining two clauses
**So that** joins an action to its **result**. The second clause normally takes a **modal** — *could,
would, can, might* — or a negative **would**.

::: rule 🧩 the two halves
first clause + **so that** + clause with a modal

_She wrote the address down **so that** she could find it later._

_the action_ · _the outcome it made possible_
:::

::: examples
- I set an alarm ==so that I wouldn't oversleep==.
- She left a note ==so that nobody would worry==.
- He turned the volume down ==so that we could talk==.
- We booked early ==so that we would get== cheaper seats.
- I kept the receipt ==so that I could return== the jacket.
- She repeated it twice ==so that I might remember== it.
:::

The negative form uses the modal every time: _so that she **wouldn't worry**_, never _so that
she didn't worry_.

::: trap
✗ ~~I rang them for find out the price.~~
✓ I rang them ==to find out== the price.
✗ ~~I need time for make a decision.~~
✓ I need time ==to make== a decision.
✗ ~~Can you lend me money to a taxi?~~
✓ Can you lend me money ==for a taxi==?
✗ ~~I set an alarm so that I don't oversleep.~~
✓ I set an alarm so that I ==wouldn't oversleep==.
✗ ~~She spoke quietly so that nobody will hear.~~
✓ She spoke quietly so that nobody ==could hear==.
**For** takes a noun, **to** takes a verb, and the **so that** clause wants a **modal** — not a
plain past.
:::

::: cards
# 🎯 purpose with to
_I rang **to check** the listing._
# 📦 purpose with for
_I queued **for** the tickets._
# 🔗 result with so that
_I set an alarm **so that I wouldn't oversleep**._
# ⚖️ for vs to
_a queue **for** tickets · an hour **to** queue_
:::

::: summary
- **to + verb** gives the purpose of an action: _I rang **to check**._
- **for** + noun, **to** + verb: _late **for** the meeting_, _a reason **to** leave_.
- **so that** joins two clauses; the second takes a modal — _so that she **could** find it_.
- The negative **so that** clause keeps the modal: _so that nobody **would** worry_.
:::
