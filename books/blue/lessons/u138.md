## in and out: crossing a boundary

**in** goes in, **out** comes out. The pair works with almost any movement verb.

::: compare
# ⬇️ in — crossing inwards
- How did the thieves get ==in==?
- I left the back door open, so you can let yourself ==in==.
- She dived ==in==.
- I'm moving ==in== on Friday.
- We checked ==in== at the airport.
# ⬆️ out — crossing outwards
- Stay ==in== the car. Don't get ==out==.
- She had no key, so the flat was ==locked out from her== — no: she was ==locked out==.
- She climbed ==out==.
- He moved ==out== in April.
- We checked ==out== at six.
:::

==== Also: go in, come in, walk in, break in — and go out, get out, move out, let somebody out.

## in or into, out or out of

You can leave **into** or **out of** in, for more detail.

::: compare
# 📦 in / out — short, neutral
- I'm moving ==in== on Friday.
- She climbed ==out==.
# 📦 into / out of — the boundary named
- I'm moving ==into the new flat in Brest== on Friday.
- She climbed ==out of the pool==.
:::

## A handful of verbs + in

::: compare
# 👋 drop in — visit without arranging it
- I ==dropped in to see== Marta on my way home.
# 🃏 join in — join something already going on
- A game of chess was going on, so I ==joined in==.
# 🔌 plug in — connect to the electricity
- The fridge won't work: you haven't ==plugged it in==.
# 🎭 take in — deceive
- He said he was a policeman, and I was completely ==taken in==.
# 📝 fill in / fill out — write on a form
- Please ==fill in the application== and send it by Friday.
:::

## A handful of verbs + out

::: compare
# 🍽️ eat out — eat at a restaurant
- There was nothing at home, so we ==ate out==.
# 📚 drop out — leave before finishing
- Anya began a degree but ==dropped out== in her second year.
# 😅 get out of — avoid what you agreed to
- I said I'd go to the party, and now I have to find a way out.
# ✂️ leave out — do not include
- You can ==leave out== the word 'that' in that sentence.
# ➗ cross out — draw a line through
- Two of the names had been ==crossed out of the list==.
:::

==== **Drop in** and **drop out** share a verb and do opposite things: go in unannounced, or go out before the end.

::: trap
✗ I was locked ==out to== the house all night.
✓ I was locked ==out of the house== all night.
✗ Please ==fill in to== the form and send it.
✓ Please ==fill in the form== and send it.
✗ He was ==taken in in== the advert.
✓ He was ==taken in by== the advert.
✗ I promised to go, but I can't ==get out it==.
✓ I promised to go, and now I can't get out of the reception.
✗ She moved ==out to== her flat last month.
✓ She moved ==out of her flat== last month.
✗ The class ==joined in to== a game of football.
✓ The class ==joined in with== a game of football.
✗ I ==filled in with== water to the brim.
✓ I ==filled it with== water to the brim.
:::

::: summary
- **in** crosses a boundary inwards, **out** comes out: _get in, get out, check in, check out._
- Add **into** or **out of** when the boundary is worth naming: _climb out of the pool._
- With **in**: _drop in_ (unannounced visit), _join in_, _plug in_, _take in_ (deceive), _fill in_.
- With **out**: _eat out_, _drop out_, _get out of_, _leave out_, _cross out_.
:::
