::: scene 🗓️ Two messages from Rita
Rita is moving flats. She leaves a note for her brother.
> Rita: The van leaves on Sunday. Everything must be out of the house ==by Friday==.
> Dan: And how long are you staying in the new place?
> Rita: ==Until== the end of the year, probably. Well — ==by then== I'll know for sure.
> Dan: What time is the last train?
> Rita: Half past ten. We must be home ==by ten==.
:::

## by = not later than

**by** puts a deadline on an event: the event happens *on that day, or earlier*.

::: form
# 📮 by + a point in time
by Monday · by 5 o'clock · by 14 August · by the end of the year
Let me know ==by Friday== whether you can come.
Please make sure you're here ==by 2 o'clock==.
:::

A useful paraphrase: ==by Saturday== means ==not later than Saturday==.

## until = how long it goes on

**until** (or **till**) marks the end of a period. The situation carries on right up to that point — and with **not**, it does not start before it.

::: examples
- Joe will be away ==until Monday==.
- Wait ==until it stops raining==.
- She stayed in bed ==until half past ten==.
- He didn't get up ==until half past ten==.
:::

**until** may be followed by a clause: _wait until it stops._ **by** takes a noun or a time, not a clause.

## The pair side by side

::: compare
# ⏳ until — the situation continues
- Joe will be away ==until Monday==. ==(so he'll be back on Monday)==
- I'll be working ==until 11.30==. ==(so I'll stop at 11.30)==
- The shop is open ==until 6==. ==(then it closes)==
# ✅ by — the event happens, not later than
- Joe will be back ==by Monday==.
- I'll have finished the report ==by 11.30==.
- The shop closes ==by 6==. ==(sooner if it likes)==
:::

The last pair is the sharp one: _open until six_ says the shop is open **right up to** six, while _close by six_ only says it is shut **by** then.

## by the time …

**By the time** introduces the later of two events. The clause after it carries the second one — usually the **past perfect** in a past situation.

::: timeline
span past now | the car breaks down, and then the guests go home
point past+6 | Karen's car breaks down on the way
point past+32 | by the time she arrives, most guests have left
:::

::: examples
- Come on! ==By the time== the bus leaves, we still won't have found a taxi.
- ==By the time== the waiter brought the bill, we had missed the last bus.
- ==By the time== I put the books away, it was time to go to bed.
:::

In the past you can shorten it: _by then_, _by that time_ — _by midnight most of the guests had gone._

::: summary
- **by** + a time = *not later than*: _by Friday, by 5 o'clock, by the end of the year._
- **until** (or **till**) + a time or clause = *how long it continues*: _wait until it stops._ **not until** = *not before*.
- Continuing **until** six ends **at** six; happening **by** six may happen earlier.
- **By the time** introduces the second of two events: _By the time she arrived, the guests had left._
:::
