## The backbone

Nearly every English sentence has the same bones: **subject → verb → object → place → time**. The middle is the part that matters most, because the verb and its object never come apart.

:::: row
::: compare
# ✅ the bones
subject | verb | object | place | time
I | met | a colleague | outside the station | —

# ❌ broken bones
I | a colleague | outside the station | met
:::

::: note 🧱
Put the **verb** first, then the **object**, then everything else. _I borrowed **some money** from a friend_, never _I borrowed from a friend some money_.

:::
::::

## Place, then time

When a sentence has both, the place comes first and the time second.

::: form
# 📍 + ⏰ the normal order
in a small village | since 2019
to school | every day
to the cinema | every Friday

# 🔄 a short time phrase can jump in front
Last night | Lisa left her umbrella in a restaurant.
This morning | I met a friend of mine.
:::

Long time phrases like _for a long time_ stay at the end. Short ones like _last night_ and _this morning_ can move to the front — and then the listener already knows when you are talking about.

## Short phrases in the middle

Adverbs of manner, small prepositional phrases and short time expressions all fit between the object and the rest.

::: examples
- Did you see Chris ==recently==?
- Sam watches TV ==all the time==.
- Dan won the race ==easily==.
- I ate my breakfast ==quickly== and went out.
- We drove ==along the coast== and then stopped for lunch.
:::

::: trap
✗ Dan won easily the race.
✓ Dan won the race ==easily==.
✗ Joe doesn't like very much football.
✓ Joe doesn't like football ==very much==.
✗ Have you seen recently Chris?
✓ Have you seen Chris ==recently==?
:::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? I borrowed from my uncle a lot of money.
= I borrowed a lot of money from my uncle.
? Did you send to the office a copy of the contract?
= Did you send a copy of the contract to the office?
? They ___ in the same house for a very long time.
= have lived
? Sarah ___ her children to school every day.
= takes
? Why ___ home so late?
= did you come
? Ben walks to work every morning.
= OK
? We read an excellent novel on the train.
= OK
? Please ___ your address on the back of the card.
= write
:::

::: summary
- The order is **verb → object → place → time**; the verb and object stay together.
- Place before time: _to school every day_.
- Short time phrases may come first: _Last night I left my umbrella…_.
- Adverbs and short phrases sit between the object and the rest.
:::
