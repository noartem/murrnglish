::: scene 🎨 The exhibition
Two friends meet at the gallery by accident.
> Bea: I didn't know the show was on. I came here **by chance**.
> Joris: What a coincidence. Was it worth the trip?
> Bea: Yes — the portraits at the back are **by** a painter I'd never heard of.
> Joris: I'd have gone **by** train, but I came **on foot**. My ticket price went **up by** a third since April.
> Bea: Well, you saved the difference.
:::

## by + a noun: how or why

This group takes **by** with a bare noun — no **a**, no **the**.

::: form
# 🎲 by + a bare noun
by chance
by accident
by mistake
by hand
by email
by credit card
by post
:::

::: trap
✗ I didn't do it ==by the accident==.
✓ I didn't do it ==by accident==.
✗ We met ==by a chance==.
✓ We met ==by chance==.
✗ I didn't do it ==on purpose== — no, that one is right, but it means the opposite.
✓ I didn't do it ==on purpose==. ==(= deliberately)==
✗ She washes her hair ==by the hand==.
✓ She washes her hair ==by hand==.
:::

::: trap
✗ She washes her hair ==by the hand==.
✓ She washes her hair ==by hand==.
:::

## by + a vehicle: how you travel

With a vehicle **by** says *using that means*, with no article.

::: compare
# 🚗 by — the means
- I go to work ==by bus==.
- She travels ==by train==, then ==by tube==.
# 🚗 in / on — a particular one
- She came ==in her car==.
- We went ==on the 6.45 train==.
# 🚶 on foot
- We walked. ==(on foot, not by foot)==
:::

No **by** with a car or taxi as a vehicle word, and no **by** with **my**, **the** or **a**: ~~by my car~~, ~~by taxi~~.

Two more things travel with **by**: pay **by** card, send it **by** post — but you pay **cash**.

## by + an agent: somebody did it

The passive **by** names who or what performed the action.

::: examples
- The window was broken ==by a boy from next door==.
- The bridge was designed ==by a young engineer==.
- Has that window ever been broken ==by a stone==?
:::

by or with?

The **instrument** (the tool you used) takes **with**; the **doer** takes **by**.

::: compare
# 🔧 with — the tool
- The door was opened ==with a key==. ==(somebody used a key)==
- She was hit ==with a stick==.
# 👤 by — the person
- The door was opened ==by a man with a key==.
- The picture was painted ==by a woman in her thirties==.
:::

## by = beside

**by** a person or a thing means **next to** it.

::: examples
- The light switch is ==by the door==.
- Come and sit ==by me==.
- There is a small table ==by the bed== with a lamp ==on== it.
:::

## by = the difference between two things

**by** also measures a gap: how much more, how much further, how much earlier.

::: form
# 📏 by + an amount
increased by ten per cent
won by five metres
missed her by five minutes
cut the journey time by two hours
:::

- The population has risen ==by six million==.
- She beat me ==by two seconds==.
- I missed the last bus ==by a minute==.

::: summary
- **by** + a bare noun says *how* or *why*: _by chance, by hand, by email, by post, by card._
- For travel, **by** has no article and no **my**: _by bus, by train, by bike, on foot._
- In the passive, **by** names the doer and **with** the tool: _opened by a man with a key._
- **by** also means *next to* and *the difference between*: _by the door, up by eight per cent._
:::
