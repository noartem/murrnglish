::: scene 🔐 The club door
Three friends at the entrance, and three ways of saying the same condition.
> Pia: You can't come in ==unless== you've got a jacket.
> Otto: You may use my van ==as long as== you fill it with petrol afterwards.
> Bea: I'll lend you the money ==provided== you pay it back on Friday.
:::

## unless = if … not

**unless** joins two clauses and means *if not*. The main verb is then negative.

:::: row
::: compare
# 🔁 with a negative in the other clause
- You won't pass ==if you don't try== a bit harder.
- I'll go ==if it doesn't rain==.

# 🔁 with unless
- You won't pass ==unless you try== a bit harder.
- I'll go ==unless it rains==.
:::

::: note 🧩
**unless** needs the other clause to be positive. _Unless you **don't** study_ means "if you study", which is not what anyone means.
:::
::::

## unless at the start

Swap the two halves and you get a **conditional** clause at the front, with a comma after it.

::: form
# 🔄 one order
You won't get in unless you pay. | You won't get in if you don't pay.

# 🔄 the other order
Unless you pay, you won't get in.
Unless it stops raining, we'll cancel.
:::

## as long as and provided

These mean **if and only if** — the condition is not just necessary, it is the deal.

::: compare
# 🤝 the condition is the deal
- I'll lend it to you ==as long as== you return it by Friday.
- I'll lend it to you ==provided you return it== by Friday.

# 🚪 not being open to the public
- Children are allowed in ==provided they are with an adult==.
:::

**Provided that** and **providing that** are the long forms. At the start of a sentence, **Provided** is written with a capital letter and normally takes **that**: _Provided (that) you pay, I'll go._

## Which one?

::: compare
# ❌ only if not
- The club isn't open to everyone. You are allowed in ==only if you are a member==.
- You'll miss the train ==unless you leave now==.

# 🤝 if, and the condition matters
- You can take the car ==as long as== you are insured.
- I'll wait ==provided== you come.
:::

Ask two questions. Is there a negative in the first clause? Use **unless**. Otherwise, is it a fair condition? Use **as long as** or **provided**.

::: trap
✗ You won't fail unless you don't revise.
✓ You won't fail ==unless you revise==.
✗ Unless you don't hurry, you'll be late.
✓ ==If you don't hurry==, you'll be late.
✗ I'll help you as long as you will ask me.
✓ I'll help you ==as long as you ask me==.
:::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? You can't enter ___ you have a ticket.
= unless
? The dog won't chase you ___ you run.
= unless
? I'll lend you the book ___ you bring it back.
= provided
? I'll lend you the book ___ you don't damage it.
= as long as
? ___ you come back early, we'll wait.
= Unless
? I don't mind the heat ___ it isn't humid.
= as long as
? Children may use the pool ___ an adult is with them.
= provided
? She won't come ___ nobody invites her.
= unless
? Take a coat ___ it turns cold.
= in case
:::

::: summary
- **unless** = **if … not**, with a positive second clause.
- At the start, **Unless** is followed by a comma: _Unless you pay, you won't get in._
- **as long as** and **provided (that)** = a condition that is the whole deal.
- Negative situation → **unless**; fair condition → **as long as** or **provided**.
:::
