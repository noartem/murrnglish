> Nadia: I really ==enjoy going== to the cinema.
> Cleo: I don't. I ==prefer watching== something at home.
> Bo: I ==love cooking==, but tonight I ==don't fancy cooking== at all.
> Nadia: ==Would you like to come== over?
> Bo: I would, but I ==hate washing up== afterwards.
> Cleo: I ==would love to have gone== out tonight, actually.

## In general, or this once
The plain verbs are the flexible ones: **like**, **love**, **hate**, **enjoy**, **don't mind**, **prefer** take either form, and the meaning shifts with it.

::: form
# 🎯 -ing = in general · to = this time, or a choice
I enjoy ==walking==. | I enjoy it. (as a habit)
I enjoy ==to walk==. | I enjoy it. (the same idea, no difference)
I'd like ==walking==. | Walking is what I like.
I'd like ==to walk==. | Walking is what I want now.
:::

::: cards
# 😊 in general
- I ==enjoy cooking== for friends.
- She ==likes working== with children.
- They ==don't mind waiting==.
- He ==hates getting up== early.
# 🎯 this once
- I'd like ==to eat== out tonight.
- We ==love to travel== in winter.
- She ==doesn't want to cook== again.
- Do you fancy ==going== for a drink?
:::

When both forms mean the same thing — plain **like** and **enjoy** — choose the one that sounds
better. With **would like**, **hate** and **prefer**, the difference is real and worth keeping.

::: compare
# 🗓️ a general taste
- She ==likes taking== photographs.
- I ==don't like being== indoors all day.
# 🎯 a plan for now
- I ==like to be== home before seven.
- He ==doesn't like to eat== this late.
:::

## would love / would hate — a regret about the past
After **would** these verbs can reach backwards with **have + past participle** — a wish about
something that did not happen.

::: rule 🧩 one step further back
would like / love / hate **+ to have + done**

_I would love **to have gone** with you._

_the wish_ · _the thing that didn't happen_
:::

::: examples
- ==I would love to have seen== that programme.
- ==I would hate to have lost== my watch.
- ==I wouldn't like to have been== alone.
- ==We'd love to have known== about the delay.
- ==I'd rather have stayed== home.
:::

## don't mind, would prefer
A third pair sits alongside them, and it is about what **doesn't bother** you.

::: examples
- I ==don't mind waiting== a bit.
- ==I'd rather eat== at home tonight.
- ==I'd prefer to eat== at home tonight.
- ==Do you mind if I open== a window?
:::

::: note 🍽️
**Would you mind…?** and **Would you like…?** sound identical but are opposites. _Would you mind
**opening** the window?_ expects no. _Would you like **to open** the window?_ expects yes.
:::

::: trap
✗ ~~I enjoy to cook.~~
✓ I enjoy ==cooking==.
✗ ~~She doesn't like to be alone.~~
✓ She doesn't like ==being== alone. (both are fine — pick the one that sounds natural)
✗ ~~I would love having seen it.~~
✓ I would love ==to have seen== it. (past) / I would love ==to see== it. (still possible)
✓ Would love ==to see== it — but only if it hasn't finished yet.
After **would**, the wish about the past needs **to have done** — not **having done**.
:::

::: cards
# 🗓️ planned
_I'd like **to go** to the coast._
# ♾️ general
_I like **going** to the coast._
# ⏪ a wish about the past
_I would love **to have gone**._
:::

::: summary
- **like, love, hate, enjoy, don't mind + -ing** = in general; **+ to do** = this time or a
  plan.
- With plain **like / enjoy** the two often mean the same; with **would like / hate / prefer**
  the difference is real.
- **would love / hate / prefer + to have done** = a wish about something that did not happen.
- **Would you mind…?** expects no; **Would you like…?** expects yes.
:::
