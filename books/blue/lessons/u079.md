## Words with two parts

Some nouns name **two** of something, so they are plural even in the singular sense.

::: compare
# 👖 always plural
- ==My trousers== are too long.
- ==Those jeans== look great on you.
- I need ==some new pyjamas==.
- Where are ==my glasses==?
- I can't find ==my binoculars==.
# ✂️ not with a plural verb
- My new glasses ==doesn't== fit.
- These scissors ==aren't== sharp.
:::

With these, either use **a pair of** or keep the plural verb: _a pair of jeans_ (singular) or _My jeans are too long_ (plural).

## The same in both

::: form
# 🔄 singular or plural
means | one means of transport · many means of transport
series | a TV series · two TV series
species | one species of bird · hundreds of species
:::

**news** is different: it is uncountable and singular — _The news was unexpected._

## Groups of people

Nouns that name a **group** often take a **plural verb**, because we think of the members.

::: form
# 👥 group nouns
audience · committee · family · firm · government · staff · team · police

The government ==have== raised taxes. (they)
The staff ==are== unhappy. (they)
The police ==are== investigating. (they)
:::

A singular verb is possible too: _The government **wants** to change the law._

**persons** is not used. Say **people**: _They're nice people_, never _nice persons._

## One thing, not many

When we add up money, time or distance, we treat the whole as **one** thing, so the verb stays singular.

::: examples
- Fifty thousand pounds ==was== stolen. (= it)
- Three years ==is== a long time without work.
- Two miles ==isn't== far to walk.
- Ten kilos ==is== a lot for one person.
:::

::: summary
- Two-part nouns are plural: _trousers, jeans, pyjamas, glasses, binoculars, scissors_.
- Use _a pair of_ or a plural verb — never both.
- _means, series, species_ work either way; **news** is singular.
- Group nouns like _government, staff, police_ often take a plural verb.
- Sums, periods and distances are one thing: _Fifty thousand pounds was stolen._
:::
