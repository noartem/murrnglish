Jonas recognises a friend from a photo on the wall.
> Jonas: Is that you at the café in Hill Street?
> Kate: Yes! It opened last month. ==There's== a queue outside every weekend now.
> Jonas: Really? I passed it yesterday and the sign said ==there had been== a delay.
> Kate: ==There used to be== a shop there. Now ==there's== a car park next door.
:::

## Something new, or something known

**there** introduces a thing into the story. **it** points at something already identified.

::: compare
# 🆕 there — saying it exists
- ==There's== a new café in Hill Street.
- The train was stopped for ages. ==There was== a lot of traffic.
- Things have got dearer. ==There has been== a big rise in prices.
# 🎯 it — the thing we know
- We went to the new café. ==It's== very good.
- I wasn't expecting her to call. ==It was== a complete surprise.
- ==There might be== a flight tonight. I'll check.
:::

**There** can also stand for *in that place*, as in _the spare room — nobody sleeps there_.

## Guesswork about there

::: form
# 🔮 guessing
there will be · there might be · there may be
there must be · there must have been
there should be · there shouldn't be
there is sure to be · there is bound to be
there used to be · there is going to be
:::

::: trap
✗ The classroom faces the motorway. It must be constant din.
✓ The classroom faces the motorway. ==There must be== constant din.
✗ That building is a supermarket now. It used to be a cinema.
✓ That building is a supermarket now. ==It used to be== a cinema.
:::

## It at the start of a sentence

For **it + adjective**, the usual order is reversed: _Walking in the road is dangerous_ becomes _**It's dangerous to walk in the road.**_

**It** also opens sentences about space, about how long ago, and about the sky:

_How far is it from the office?_ · _It is ages since we last spoke._

::: examples
- ==It's dangerous== to swim after dark here.
- ==It didn't take== us long to reach the harbour.
- ==It's no good== waiting any longer — let's go.
- ==How far is it== from the station?
:::

? They must be at home. ___ must be somebody there.

:::

::: summary
- **There** = saying something exists: _There's a new café in Hill Street_.
- **It** = the thing you already know: _We went there. It's good_.
- Guessing about existence: _there might be, there must be, there used to be_.
- _It's dangerous to walk in the road_ — it goes in front of the adjective.
:::
