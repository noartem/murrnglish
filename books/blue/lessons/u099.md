## The queue of adjectives

English does not simply pile adjectives up. When there are two or three, they stand in a fixed order. You do not have to learn every slot — but opinion first, then size, then the rest, is enough for almost everything.

::: compare
# 📐 the usual order
opinion · size · shape or quality · age · colour · origin · material · purpose
- ==a lovely small Italian café==
- ==a new black leather wallet==
- ==a huge round wooden table==

# 🌀 and a normal one
- ==a tired child==
- ==a dark room==
:::

The last slot, **purpose**, is where things go that are made for a job: a ==knife== is for cutting, so it can be a ==cutting knife==; a ==shirt== for summer is a ==summer shirt==.

## Verbs that want an adjective

A small group of verbs is never followed by an action — they are followed by an **adjective**, because the verb is really a form of _be_.

::: form
# 👀 the five senses + seem
feel · look · seem · smell · sound · taste
→ an adjective, not an adverb

# 🗣️ examples
It ==tastes== awful. · The flowers ==smell== nice.
You ==look== wet. · It ==sounds== interesting.
:::

::: trap
✗ It tastes badly.
✓ It ==tastes bad==.
✗ You look sadly.
✓ You ==look sad==.
✗ The soup smells very nicely.
✓ The soup ==smells very nice==.
:::

## Adjective or adverb?

An **adjective** goes before a noun or after a linking verb. An **adverb** goes with a verb that does something.

::: compare
# 🏷️ adjective
- a ==happy== child
- She looks ==happy==.
- I feel ==terrible== today.

# 🏃 adverb
- The children were playing ==happily==.
- He explained everything ==clearly==.
- Please drive ==slowly==.
:::

Some words are both: _fast, hard, late, early, straight, high, well_ — and you choose by the job the word is doing.

## The first, the next, the last

To count a group in words, put **the first / the next / the last** in front of a **plural** noun. The verb takes the singular.

::: form
# 🔢 counting
the ==first two days== of the course
the ==next three weeks==
the ==last two years==

# ✏️ the verb is singular
the first two days ==were== wet
:::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? The flat at the top of the building is ___ and ___.
= small / modern
? She sang ___ and the hall went quiet.
= beautifully
? This soup ___ a bit odd.
= tastes
? The ___ two questions were the easy ones.
= first
? He explained everything very ___ , thank you.
= clearly
? The bread we bought yesterday ___ delicious.
= tastes
? I saw that film ___ , so I can’t remember it.
= already
? These trousers don’t ___ me any more.
= fit
? We walked ___ along the wet pavement.
= quickly
:::

::: summary
- Adjectives queue up: opinion, size, shape, age, colour, origin, material, purpose.
- **feel, look, seem, smell, sound, taste** take an adjective, not an adverb.
- An adjective sits before a noun or after a linking verb; an adverb goes with an action.
- **the first / the next / the last** take a plural noun and a singular verb.
:::
