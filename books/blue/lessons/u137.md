## What a phrasal verb is

A verb plus a small second word — **in, on, up, out, off, down, away, back, through, over, round** — and the pair carries its own meaning.

::: words
in · on · up · down
out · off · away · back
round · over · through · along
forward · about · by
:::

Most of them come with verbs of movement:

::: examples
- We couldn't ==get on== the bus.
- She got into the car and ==drove off==.
- He ==came back== on Saturday.
- When I tapped him on the arm he ==turned round==.
:::

## The second word changes the meaning

::: compare
# 🔧 the plain verb — the machine simply stopped
- The car ==broke down== on the motorway. ==(the engine stopped working)==
# 🔍 find out — you discovered it
- I never ==found out== who sent the flowers.
# ✈️ take off — a plane left the ground
- The plane ==took off== in thick fog.
# 🙌 give up — you stopped trying
- After a month I ==gave up== trying to reach her.
# 📈 get on — how you managed
- How did you ==get on== in the exam?
# 💸 get by — just enough to manage
- My French isn't perfect, but it's enough to ==get by==.
:::

## A third preposition on the end

Some phrasal verbs take a preposition after them.

::: form
# 🔗 phrasal verb + preposition
look up at
run away from
keep up with
look forward to
:::

## Two positions for a noun

A noun object can go before or after the phrasal verb.

::: compare
# 📦 both positions work
- ==I turned on the light==.
- or ==I turned the light on==.
- ==I'm going to take off my shoes==.
- or ==I'm going to take my shoes off==.
# 🙅 the second position is the only one
- ==Please switch the radio off==.
- but not ~~switch off the radio is fine only for nouns~~
:::

## Pronouns stay at the end

With **it, them, me, him, her** there is only one place for them: after the whole verb.

::: trap
✗ I turned ==on it==.
✓ I turned ==it on==.
✗ Please don't ==throw away it== — I want to keep it.
✓ Please don't ==throw it away== — I want to keep it.
✗ It's midnight and the child is asleep; don't ==wake up her==.
✓ It's midnight and the child is asleep; don't ==wake her up==.
✗ These new boots pinch; I'm going to ==take off them==.
✓ These new boots pinch; I'm going to ==take them off==.
:::

::: summary
- A phrasal verb is a verb plus a small second word: _get on, drive off, break down, find out, give up._
- The second word often changes the meaning entirely: a car **breaks down**, a plane **takes off**, you **give up**.
- A noun object can sit on either side: _turn on the light_ / _turn the light on._
- A pronoun object only goes at the end: _turn **it** on_ — never _turn on it_.
:::
