## When you need of

**all, some, any, most, none** can take a noun directly — but once you add **the, my, these, those**, English wants **of**.

::: compare
# ❌ no of
- ==Some people== learn faster than others.
- ==Most problems== have a solution.
- ==All flowers== are beautiful.

# ✅ with of
- ==Some of the tenants== complained about the heating.
- We solved ==most of the problems== we had.
- ==All of the flowers== in the garden died.
:::

After **all** and **half**, **of** is optional: _All my friends live here_ or _All of my friends live here._

## it, us, you, them

With these pronouns **of** is always needed.

::: examples
- ==All of us== were late.
- I've only read ==half of it==.
- ==Some of them== can't come to the party.
- Do ==any of you== want tea?
:::

## On its own

Without a following noun, the quantifier stands alone.

::: examples
- Some trams are double-decked and ==some== are single.
- A few of the shops were shut, but ==most== were open.
- ==Half== the takings are mine; the company keeps the ==half== that remains.
:::

::: note 🎯
Compare: _All flowers are beautiful_ = every flower there is.
_All of these flowers are beautiful_ = only the ones in front of you.
:::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? I spend much ___ my free time reading.
= of
? ___ of the players played brilliantly.
= Most
? We all got soaked because ___ had an umbrella.
= none of us
? I hadn't finished the novel. I'd read ___ of it.
= half
? Do you want any ___ these biscuits?
= of
:::

::: summary
- Add **of** after a determiner: _some of the people, most of my time_.
- Never with a bare plural: _some people_, not ~~some of people~~.
- Always before it / us / you / them: _all of it, none of us_.
- Without a noun, the quantifier stands alone: _some have two doors_.
:::
