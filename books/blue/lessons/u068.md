## The second action
An **-ing clause** adds a second action to a sentence. It has **no subject of its own**, and it
usually sits **at the end**, after the comma.

::: rule 🧩 the shape
main clause + **-ing** clause

_Joe wrenched his ankle **falling off the wall**._

_the main news_ · _how it happened_
:::

The **-ing** clause normally comes last. When it comes earlier, set it off with a **comma**:
_Arriving late, we slipped in without a sound._

::: examples
- I ==ended up soaked cycling== home in the rain.
- Laura ==put her car through a hedge== swerving round a corner.
- My friend ==left his wallet on== the bus.
- Emily ==put her back out moving== a chest of drawers alone.
- Two people were ==overcome by fumes trying to clear== the flat.
- He ==kept the whole thing quiet rather than cause== a fuss.
:::

## The -ing clause with a subject
When the extra action belongs to somebody else, or runs alongside the first, the clause carries
its **own subject**.

::: form
# 👥 a subject inside the clause
- **Subject** + **-ing** + subject + verb

_Rosa went out **promising to be back** before midnight._

_two subjects_ · _what each one did_
:::

::: examples
- Nils was in the workshop ==putting a bicycle back together==.
- Priya sat on the wall ==feeding the pigeons==.
- The inspector walked in ==holding a clipboard== and asked for the log.
- My aunt worked in Leeds ==teaching physics==.
- We drove round the coast ==stopping at every harbour==.
- The room was cold, ==so we put the heating on==.

::: note ⚖️
**Nils was putting a bicycle back together** — one subject, two actions at once.
**Nils was in the workshop putting a bicycle back together** — the workshop says *where*.
Both are right; the extra subject carries information the first verb alone doesn't.
:::

## Not -ing
A negative **-ing** clause at the front explains **why** something was impossible or awkward. It
always takes a **comma** after it.

::: rule 🧩 the negative frame
**Not** + -ing + subject + verb, + the rest

_Not having a passport_, she couldn't fly.

_the missing thing_ · _what it cost you_
:::

::: examples
- ==Not having studied the map==, we took a wrong turn twice.
- ==Not having met her before==, he said something awkward.
- ==Not being able to read the form==, she asked for help.
- ==Not liking the pace==, he handed the ticket back.
- ==Being a vegetarian==, Robert never orders the fish here.

(The last one is a good contrast: **being** is positive, and the negative arrives later.)::

## Having done
**Having + past participle** puts the **earlier finished action** first. It is the formal
alternative to putting **after** at the start of the second clause.

::: form
# ⏮️ earlier, and finished
**Having** + past participle + subject + verb

_Having missed the last bus_, they walked home.

_the earlier action_ · _what followed_
:::

::: examples
- ==Having paid already==, she asked for the receipt.
- ==Having read the brief==, Tomas had three questions.
- ==Having apologised==, she found it hard to ask for anything else.
- ==Having been warned about the traffic==, he left at six.
- ==Having spent most of his life abroad==, Sam now finds England cramped.
- ==Having lost her licence==, Joanna took a bus to work.

## Two sentences, one clause
Both fronted patterns exist to **join** two sentences, and the comma is what marks the join.

::: compare
# 🔗 two sentences
- My ankle was sore. So I stayed in.
- ==With a sore ankle,== I stayed in.
- I thought they might need feeding. So I put the plates out.
- ==Thinking they might need feeding,== I put the plates out.
# ✅ the fronted clause
- Elias has sailed a lot. So he knows the ropes.
- ==Having sailed a lot,== Elias knows the ropes.
:::

::: trap
✗ ~~He hurt his knee to play football.~~
✓ He hurt his knee ==playing== football.
✗ ~~Not have a passport, she could not fly.~~
✓ ==Not having== a passport, she could not fly.
✗ ~~Having paid already she asked for the bill.~~
✓ ==Having paid already,== she asked for the bill.
✗ ~~Not having a phone I had no way to call.~~
✓ ==Not having a phone,== I had no way to call.
✗ ~~Not having a key she slept in the hall.~~
✓ ==Not having a key,== she slept in the hall.
The extra action is **-ing**, never **to** + verb — and a fronted **-ing** or **Having** clause
takes a **comma** after it.
:::

::: note 🖊️
That comma is the only thing separating the fronted clause from the sentence proper, and it is what
makes the sentence readable. Drop it and the reader has to guess where the clause ends — _Not
having a key she slept in the hall_ is genuinely hard to parse.
:::

::: summary
- An **-ing clause** adds a second action, usually at the end: _He wrenched his ankle **falling off the wall**._
- It may carry its own subject: _She went out **promising to be back**._
- **Not -ing** at the front explains what was missing — with a comma.
- **Having + done** at the front gives the earlier completed action — also with a comma.
- The second action is **-ing**, never **to** + verb.
:::
