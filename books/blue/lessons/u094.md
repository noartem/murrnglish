## whose

**whose** shows who something belongs to. It goes in front of a noun, and it works with **people and things**.

::: compare
# 👤 people
- a man ==whose wife is an English teacher==
- somebody ==whose mother writes detective stories==
- a couple ==whose ambition is to climb Everest==

# 📦 things
- a country ==whose exports are all coffee==
- a book ==whose last chapter is the best one==
:::

It is the same idea as _the wife of that man_ — only shorter, and never wrong in speech.

::: examples
- The house ==whose garden is full of roses== sells quickly.
- That is the girl ==whose essay won the prize==.
:::

## whom, who, or nothing

To the **object** of a clause about a person, the word may be left out altogether. In writing you can put **whom** in front of it. **who** and **that** are the everyday forms.

::: compare
# 🎩 more formal
- the lawyer ==whom a colleague recommended==
- the woman ==with whom Tom was in love==
- the person ==to whom I lent the money==

# 🧢 less formal
- the lawyer ==a colleague recommended==
- the woman ==Tom was in love with==
- the person ==I lent the money to==
:::

::: note 📌
**whom** is never used for a thing, and never in the subject position. If the clause does the acting — the man ==who phoned== — use **who**, not **whom**.
:::

## where

A clause about a **place** uses **where**, or a preposition + which.

::: compare
# 📍 where
- the village ==where everybody knows everybody else==
- the hotel ==where we stayed last summer==
- a cemetery ==is a place where people are buried==

# 🔁 preposition + which
- the town ==in which I grew up==
- the street ==where she bought the bread==
- the park ==which runs along the river==
:::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? I met a woman ___ son is a famous actor.
= whose
? This is the café ___ we met for lunch.
= where
? The book ___ cover I tore is in the bin.
= whose
? The lawyer ___ I spoke to could not help me.
= whom
? She lives in a flat ___ the rent is very low.
= where
? I know a shop ___ everything is half price.
= where
? The team ___ won the cup has gone home.
= that
:::

::: summary
- **whose** shows who owns something, and works for people and things: a man ==whose wife teaches==.
- **whom** is the formal object word; **who**, **that** and leaving it out are the everyday versions.
- **where** points at a place: the hotel ==where we stayed==; a preposition + **which** also works.
- A clause doing the acting needs **who**: the man ==who phoned==, never _whom_.
:::
