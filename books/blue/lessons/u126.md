::: scene 🛬 A long journey
Pavel is meeting his cousin at the airport.
> Courier: The parcel is going ==to Lisbon== this afternoon.
> Pavel: It has already gone? But the address is ==in Lisbon==.
> Courier: Then you can say we're taking it ==to Lisbon==. Think of it that way.
> Pavel: Fine. My train arrives ==at the station== at six, and I get ==into== a taxi from there.
:::

## to = movement; in or at = position

**to** is for going somewhere. **in** or **at** is for being there.

::: compare
# ➡️ to — going
- They're going ==to France== next month.
- Can you come ==to the party==?
- Welcome ==to our country==.
- We cancelled our trip ==to Paris==.
# 📍 in / at — being there
- They live ==in France==.
- I'll see you ==at the party==.
:::

Those two verbs go with **to** as well: _go to work, come to my house, drive to the airport._ And **been to** reports past experience: _I've been to Rome twice; I've never been to Rome._ ==(never been there)==

## arrive in or arrive at

**arrive** never takes **to**. Which of the other two you use depends on the place.

::: form
# 🏙️ arrive in — a town or a country
They arrived ==in Madrid==.
They arrived ==in Spain== on Tuesday.
# 🏢 arrive at — a building or an event
We waited a long time ==at the hotel== for our room.
We arrived ==at the party== very late.
:::

Same rule with **get**: _get to the station._ (no **in** before a building, either).

## home takes no preposition

::: form
# 🏠 home — bare
go home
come home
get home
arrive home
on my way home
:::

::: trap
✗ What time did you get ==to home==?
✓ What time did you get ==home==?
✗ When are you going back ==in Italy==?
✓ When are you going back ==to Italy==?
✗ They arrived ==to Barcelona== on Sunday.
✓ They arrived ==in Barcelona== on Sunday.
:::

## into, and out of

**into** = crossing a boundary inwards. **out of** = crossing it outwards.

::: timeline
span now future | outside → inside → outside again
arrow now now+30 | out of the car
arrow now+32 now+40 | into the bookshop
arrow now+60 now+62 | out of the bookshop
:::

- A wasp came ==into the hall== through the open door.
- He climbed ==out of the pool== and dried himself off.
- The money goes straight ==into my savings account==.

With **get**, **go** and **put** you often use plain **in** instead: _She got **in** the car; I put the letter back **in** the envelope._

## get on and get off

For a bus, a train or a plane you get **on** and **off**. For a car or taxi you get **in** and **out**.

::: form
# 🚌 on / off — public transport
get on the bus
get off the train
get on a plane
# 🚗 in / out — private
get in the car
get out of the taxi
get into a lift
:::

===== Getting **out of** a car but **off** a bus: you step down from a bus, you climb out of a car.

::: summary
- **to** with go, come, drive and travel = movement; **in / at** = being there.
- **arrive in** a town or country, **arrive at** a building or an event. **home** takes no preposition.
- **into** goes in, **out of** comes out; with **get** and **put**, plain **in** is usual.
- Public transport: get **on** and get **off**. A car or taxi: get **in** and get **out**.
:::
