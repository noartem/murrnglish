## The word that carries the tense
When one speaker says something and the other replies **short**, the reply repeats only the **auxiliary** — the tense, the modal, or _have_ — and drops the rest.

::: form
# 🗣️ two halves of one pair
I wasn't tired, but my friends | ==were==.
Ann likes hot weather, but she | ==doesn't==.
I've travelled little, but Gary | ==has==.
You never listen to me. — Yes, I | ==do==!
I usually work Saturdays, but last Saturday I | ==didn't==.
:::

This works with modals too, which is why the reply can be so short:

::: examples
- 'Do you think it'll rain?' — 'It ==might==. Take an umbrella.'
- She's coming, isn't she? — She ==should be==.
- Can you help? — I ==would if I could==, but I ==can't==.
:::

::: note 🪞
The rule behind it: **you have no verb to replace, so you supply only the helper.** _I was tired_ needs _was_; _I went home_ needs _did_. A modal like _might_ already works on its own.
:::

## So … / Neither … — agreeing or disagreeing
After a positive statement use **So + auxiliary**. After a negative statement use **Neither + auxiliary**. The auxiliary is the one you have just heard.

::: form
# ✅ the same, positive
I like reading. | ==So do I.==
I watched TV. | ==So did I.==
I can come early. | ==So can I.==
I want to travel. | ==So do I.==
# ❌ the same, negative
I'm not tired. | ==Neither am I.==
I won't be at home. | ==Neither will I.==
I can't go out. | ==Neither can I.==
:::

::: note 🙋
**Neither** means "not me either". In everyday speech people often say **Me neither** or **Nor me** — _"I don't drink coffee." — "Me neither."_ Both are understood.
:::

## So … ? / Neither … ? — asking back
When you **don't** share the statement, keep the auxiliary and turn it into a question, then ask for more.

::: cards
# 🔎 true for you, not for them
"I can't go out tonight." — "==Can't you?== Why not?"
# 🔎 true for you, not for them
"I'd like to live elsewhere." — "==Would you?== Where would you like to live?"
# 🙋 true for both
"I'm looking forward to the weekend." — "==So am I.== Are you doing something nice?"
# 🙋 true for both
"I love this series." — "==So do I==. Have you seen the last episode?"
:::

## Don't repeat the whole question
To answer **I think so**, **I hope not**, **I suppose so**, **I'm afraid not**, leave out the question and keep only the **helper + so / not**.

::: form
# 🔁 question → short answer
Is it going to rain? | I hope ==not==.
Will you get a pay rise? | I hope ==so==.
Does she speak Italian? | I suppose ==so==.
Do you have a room tonight? | I'm afraid ==not==.
Is Marek married? | I don't think ==so==.
:::

The helper tells the listener how sure you are, and **so / not** carries the yes or no.

::: words
I think so · I don't think so · I hope so · I hope not · I suppose so · I suppose not · I'm afraid so · I'm afraid not
:::

::: trap
✗ ~~I think is so.~~
✓ I think ==it is== so.
✗ ~~I think yes.~~
✓ I think ==so==.
✗ ~~I hope yes.~~
✓ I hope ==so== / I hope ==not==.
**So** and **not** stand alone after the helper. The verb already appeared in the question — don't say it twice.
:::

::: note 🚫
In a **negative question** the answer flips meaning (see [[u52|question tags]]): to _“Don't you want to go?”_ a **Yes** means _yes, I want to go_.
:::

::: summary
- A short reply repeats only the **auxiliary**: _I was tired_ → _I was._
- Same, positive → **So + auxiliary**; same, negative → **Neither + auxiliary**.
- Not the same → keep the auxiliary as a question: _Can't you? Why not?_
- After the question: **I think so / I hope not / I suppose so / I'm afraid not**.
- **So** and **not** stand alone — don't repeat the verb.
:::
