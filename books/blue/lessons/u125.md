::: scene 📞 Where is everybody?
It is eight in the morning and nobody answers.
> Karin: Dave isn't picking up. He should be **at work** by now.
> Ben: He's **in hospital** — he broke his ankle on Sunday.
> Karin: And Ruth? Her sister said she was **in bed** until noon.
> Ben: Ruth's **at university** now. She'll be **at** the library all afternoon.
> Karin: Shall we meet **at** the station, then?
:::

## in or at with people

Some expressions take **in**, some **at**, and there is no rule you can work out — learn them.

::: compare
# 🛏️ in — inside, held, away
- He's still **in bed**.
- She's **in hospital**.
- They're **in prison** for a crime they didn't commit.
# 🏫 at — at a place of work or study
- I'll be **at work** until half past five.
- One of them is **at university**, the other is still **at school**.
- They're **at** the hairdresser's.
:::

**home** is the exception that takes both: _I'll be **home** all evening_ / _I'll be **at home** all evening._ But an activity needs **at**: _Shall we eat **at home**?_

## at an event

**at** for anything happening at a set time and place: a party, a meeting, a concert.

::: examples
- Were there many people **at** the party?
- I saw Steve **at** a conference on Saturday.
- The meeting takes place **at** the head office.
:::

## in or at for buildings

Both are often possible; the difference is what you are thinking about — the **event** or the **building**.

::: compare
# 🎪 at — the event, or someone's building
- We had dinner ==at Helen's== last night.
- The concert took place ==at the Town Hall==.
- There was a robbery ==at the supermarket==.
# 🧱 in — the building itself
- ==The heating in Helen's house== has never worked.
- Every one of the bedrooms ==in that hotel== costs extra.
::::

Note the negative with **at**: you cannot say ~~it's cold at Helen's house~~ when you mean the building.

## in for towns, at for stops

Towns and cities take **in**. A place seen as a stop on a journey takes **at**.

::: examples
- The Louvre is a famous museum ==in Paris==.
- They live ==in a small town not far from the coast==.
- Does this train stop ==at Oxford==? ==(at the station)==
:::

Also fixed: **at the station**, **at the airport**.

## on a bus, in a car

Public transport you sit in but are "on"; a private vehicle or taxi you are "in".

::: compare
# 🚌 on — bus, train, plane, ship
- The bus was full and nobody was willing to stand.
- We sat ==on the plane== and the food was awful.
# 🚗 in — car, taxi
- Laura arrived ==in a taxi==.
- We went home ==in my car==.
:::

Two wheels take **on** too: _She rode past on her bike._

::: summary
- No rule to work out: **in** bed, **in** hospital, **in** prison; **at** work, **at** school, **at** university.
- **at** an event, and **at** for someone else's building: _dinner at Helen's_.
- **in** for the building itself, and for towns: _the rooms in the hotel, a museum in Paris._
- **on** a bus, train, plane or bike; **in** a car or taxi; **at** the station, **at** the airport.
:::
