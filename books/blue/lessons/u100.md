## Most adverbs end in -ly

An adverb says **how**, **how often**, **how much**, **when in the week**. Nine times out of ten you make it by adding **-ly** to the adjective.

::: form
# ➕ the easy sums
quick → quick==ly==
careful → careful==ly==
dangerous → dangerous==ly==
perfect → perfect==ly==
permanent → permanent==ly==

# ⚠️ three spelling traps
happy → happi==ly== (drop the y before -ly)
easy → easi==ly== (the same)
terrible → terribly (add -ly, nothing else)
:::

::: note 🧩
If the adjective already ends in **-ful**, **-less**, **-ic** or **-al**, add **-ly**: beautiful → beautifully.
:::

## The ones with no -ly

A small group never takes **-ly**. These words are *already* both.

::: compare
# 🏃 same word, two jobs
- The train arrived ==late==. (adjective: not on time)
- The train arrived ==late==. (adverb: not early)
- He works ==hard==. · She sings ==fast==.

# 🏷️ the same word before a noun
- a ==late== train · a ==fast== car · an ==early== bus
- a ==hard== question · a ==high== wall
:::

The meaning does the work. _He works hard_ has no object, so **hard** is an adverb. Put a noun after it — _a hard question_ — and now it is an adjective.

## Adjective or adverb?

Ask what is doing the action. If the verb is a linking verb (**be, feel, look, seem, stay, remain**) or a sense verb, use the adjective. If somebody performs the action, use the adverb.

::: compare
# 🏷️ adjective — nothing is done
- Maria's English is very ==fluent==.
- Everything was very quiet. There was ==complete== silence.
- I'm staying for a few weeks only; I won't be here ==permanently==. — adverb

# 🏃 adverb — somebody does it
- Maria speaks English ==fluently==.
- She cooks this meal ==specially== for you.
- He lives ==dangerously==.
:::

::: trap
✗ She explained it very clear.
✓ She explained it very ==clearly==.
✗ She sings beautiful.
✓ She sings ==beautifully==.
✗ We waited patient.
✓ We waited ==patiently==.
:::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? He explained the rule very ___ .
= clearly
? The soup tasted ___ ; I need something else.
= awful
? She ___ the room every weekend.
= cleans
? Our team plays ___ ; they train every day.
= well
? I’ve only worked here a few months. I won’t be here ___.
= permanently
? Please speak ___ — I can’t hear you.
= slowly
? The children played ___ in the garden.
= happily
? That was a ___ mistake.
= serious
? He arrived ___ and we had to start without him.
= late
:::

::: summary
- Most adverbs are the adjective + **-ly**: careful → carefully.
- Watch the spelling: happy → happily, easy → easily.
- **fast, hard, late, early, straight, high, well** do not take **-ly**.
- A linking verb wants an adjective; an action verb wants an adverb.
:::
