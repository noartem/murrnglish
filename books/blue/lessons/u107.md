::: scene 🤝 The comparison
Two colleagues talk about pay, height and the bus.
> Jo: I'm not as tall as you.
> Kit: My salary isn't as high as yours.
> Jo: I was starving, so I decided to look for somewhere cheap to eat.
:::

## as … as

The pattern never changes: **as + adjective or adverb + as**. Nothing is added and no spelling changes.

::: form
# ✅ as + adjective + as
as tall as · as expensive as · as interesting as
as old as · as difficult as · as early as

# ✅ as + adverb + as
as quickly as · as quietly as · as often as
:::

Anything can go between the two **as**: as tall as my brother, as expensive as the one we saw, as quickly as I could.

## not as … as

A negative comparison keeps both **as**: _not as tall as you_, _not as expensive as that_.

::: compare
# 🙅 not as … as
- I'm not as tall as you.
- My salary isn't as high as yours.
- This one isn't as good as the other.

# 🙂 not so … as — the same, more everyday
- I'm not so tall as you.
- This one isn't so good as the other.
:::

## Turning as … as into than

In a lot of sentences **than** can say the same thing more briefly.

::: form
# 🔁 as … as → than
as much money as you → more money ==than you==
as far as I thought → nearer ==than I thought==
as much as I used to → less ==than I used to==
as well as you know them → better ==than you know them==
:::

**than** is the only choice when the first side is shorter: you spent more money ==than me== — never _as much money as me_.

## the same as, and as as a connector

:::: row
::: compare
# 🟰 the same as
- David is ==the same age as== James.
- Her eyes are ==the same colour as== her brother's.

# 🔗 as = while / because / when
- ==As soon as== I have any news, I'll tell you.
- You can stay ==as long as== you like.
- She sings ==as well as== her sister does.
:::

::: trap
✗ Does Marek look as his uncle?
✓ Does Marek look ==like== his uncle?
✗ She is as taller than me.
✓ She is ==taller== than me.
✗ She has got as much money as me.
✓ She has got more money ==than me==.
✗ I didn't arrive as early than the others.
✓ I didn't arrive ==as early as== the others.
:::
::::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? I'm not ___ as my brother.
= as tall
? My salary isn't ___ as his.
= as high
? David and James are both 22. David is ___ age ___ James.
= the same / as
? Let me know ___ possible.
= as soon
? You can stay here ___ you like.
= as long
? I answered it ___ I could.
= as well
? We spent more money ___ they did.
= than
? The train isn't ___ as the bus.
= as fast
? She didn't arrive ___ I did.
= as early
:::

::: summary
- **as + adjective/adverb + as** says two things are equal.
- Negative: **not as … as** or **not so … as** — keep both **as**.
- With a short first side, **than** is the only option: more money ==than me==.
- **the same age as** for equality; **as soon as**, **as long as**, **as well as** as connectors.
:::
