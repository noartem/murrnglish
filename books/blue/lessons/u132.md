::: scene ☎️ The rehearsal
Three people sort out a problem before the show.
> Bea: I've called the electrician twice.
> Gus: What did you say to him?
> Bea: I apologised to him for the delay, then explained the problem to him.
> Gus: Did he answer your email?
> Bea: No. I phoned him. You can't leave out **to** there — you just phone somebody.
> Gus: Right. And I pointed at the fuse box and laughed at the electrician's report.
:::

## Verbs that need to

**to** has to be there, and it goes before the person.

::: form
# 📞 to + somebody
talk to somebody
listen to the radio
apologise to somebody
reply to an email
explain something to somebody
:::

- Can you **explain** the rule **to me**?
- They apologised **to** us **for** the noise.
- I explained **to them** why I had left.

## Verbs that need no to

This group takes the person straight after the verb.

::: compare
# ✅ no to
- I ==called the airline==. ==(not called to the airline)==
- ==Can you ask me== about it? ==(not ask to me)==
- He ==thanked me== for waiting. ==(not thanked to me)==
- She never ==replied to my message==. ==(not replied my message)==
# ✍️ the exceptions
- Write ==to== somebody: ==I wrote to her== yesterday.
- But ==reply to== an email or a letter.
:::

**Explain** and **describe** are a special case: with a person you need **to** — _explain the rule **to** them_ — and **describe** always needs **to** before what is being described.

## Verbs that need at

::: form
# 👀 at + something
look at me
have a look at the screen
glance at the clock
laugh at the joke
point a knife at me
shoot at the birds
:::

==== **Laugh at** a person, and **laugh with** someone — you laugh **at** a thing or a person, **with** a companion.

## at or to: the meaning changes

::: compare
# 🗣️ shout
- He shouted ==at me==. ==(angry, aggressive)==
- She shouted ==to me==. ==(so that I would hear)==
# 🥚 throw
- Somebody threw an egg ==at the minister==. ==(to hit him)==
- She threw the keys ==to me==. ==(for me to catch)==
:::

Same verb, two prepositions, two intentions. Ask yourself: was the person the target, or the receiver?

::: summary
- **to** with talk, listen, apologise, reply and explain: _explain it **to** me._
- No **to** with call, phone, ask, thank, answer: _call the airline, ask me._
- **at** with look, glance, laugh, point and shoot: _laugh **at** me._
- **shout at** = angrily; **shout to** = so that someone hears. **throw at** = to hit; **throw to** = to catch.
:::
