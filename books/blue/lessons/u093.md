## When the word can go

Put the clause straight after the noun, with nothing else between them. If the clause is the **object** of a verb or a preposition — the thing after _to_, _in_, _with_, _on_ — the word **who / that / which** is optional: leave it out and the sentence still works.

::: compare
# ✅ keep it
- The woman ==who lives next door== is a doctor.
- What is the name of the film ==that you saw==?

# ✂️ leave it out
- The keys ==you lost== are on the table.
- I liked the film ==we saw== last week.
:::

Two tests tell you whether the word is needed. Keep **who / that / which** when the clause identifies the noun in a question about a *person*, or when the clause is the subject. Leave it out when the noun is already the object.

::: note 🔍
Ask: *the noun is the object of what?* If of a verb or a preposition, the word may go. If the clause has its own subject — the man ==**who phoned**== — the word stays.
:::

## Prepositions at the end

A preposition that belongs to the clause goes at the **end** of it, not at the beginning. This is normal in speech and normal in writing.

::: form
# ✂️ a preposition in the wrong place
I am looking for the keys ==for which== I lost.

# 📌 at the end of the clause
I am looking for the keys ==that I lost==.
:::

::: compare
# 📌 person
- ==the friend whom I borrowed the pen from==

# 📌 thing
- ==the concert that we went to==
- ==the job that I applied for==
- ==somebody you can rely on==
:::

After a person, **whom** is the formal word: the person ==whom I borrowed the pen from==. **Who** is the everyday version; **that** is the neutral one.

::: trap
✗ This is the house in where I grew up.
✓ This is the house ==where== I grew up.
✗ We went to the wedding who we were invited.
✓ We went to the wedding ==we were invited to==.
✗ That is the friend of who I told you.
✓ That is the friend ==whom/who== I told you.
:::

## that or what

**what** stands for *the thing which*. **that** only joins a clause on to a verb, a noun or an adjective — it cannot be the thing itself.

::: compare
# 🔵 what = the thing which
- I don’t understand ==what== he said.
- Tell me ==what== you want.
- ==What== you did surprised everybody.

# 🟡 that = a joining word
- I know ==that== he is right. (not _what he is right_)
- The news ==that== she won the prize came late.
:::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? Did you find the keys ___?
= you lost
? What have you done with the money ___?
= that I gave you
? The film ___ we watched was terrible.
= we watched
? Tell me ___ bothers you.
= what
? I know ___ she is leaving next week.
= that
? She married a man ___ is a pilot.
= who
? It was the first time ___ I saw the sea.
= that
:::

::: summary
- The clause may leave **who / that / which** out when its noun is the object of a verb or a preposition: the keys ==you lost==.
- Keep the word when the clause has its own subject, or when it identifies a person in a question.
- A preposition belonging to the clause goes at its end: the job ==that I applied for==, the person ==whom I told==.
- **what** = the thing which; **that** joins a clause to the rest of the sentence.
:::
