## One thing, one a

Before a **singular countable** noun put **a** or **an**. Never leave it out.

::: trap
✗ She never wears ~~hat~~.
✓ She never wears ==a hat==.
✗ Do you want to be ~~English teacher~~?
✓ Do you want to be ==an English teacher==?
✗ Be careful of ~~dog~~.
✓ Be careful of ==the dog==.
:::

The article comes before an **adjective** too: _a nice table_, _an old friend_.

## Say what kind of thing it is

**a / an** is how you name a kind of thing or a kind of person.

::: compare
# 🔢 one
- ==A dog== is an animal.
- ==My father== is ==a doctor==.
- What ==a beautiful day==!
# 👥 more than one
- ==Dogs== are animals.
- ==My parents== are both ==doctors==.
- What ==awful shoes==!
:::

It also goes with parts of the face and with qualities: _Jack has a long nose_, _She has blue eyes_, _He's got a bad cold._

## some, when you are talking about a few

**some** goes with a **plural** noun. It can mean "a number of" or "some but not all".

::: form
# 🔢 some = a number of
I’ve seen ==some good movies== recently.
I need ==some new sunglasses==.
==Some friends of mine== are coming.

# ◐ some = some, not all
==Some children== learn very quickly.
Rain is forecast in ==some regions== tomorrow.
:::

::: note 🎬
Do not use _some_ for things in general: _I love bananas_, never _I love some bananas_.
:::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? Mark works in a bookshop. He sells ___.
= books
? Would you like to be ___ actor?
= an
? I've got sore ___ . I walked for hours.
= feet
? Don't go out without ___ umbrella.
= an
? ___ of my friends are from China.
= Some
? She's ___ excellent cook.
= an
:::

::: summary
- **a / an** before a singular countable noun — never leave it out.
- Use it to say what kind of thing or person: _a bus driver_, _an old friend_.
- In the plural the noun stands alone: _Dogs are animals_.
- **some** + plural: _some books_ (a number of) or _some places_ (not all).
- No _some_ for things in general: _I love bananas_.
:::
