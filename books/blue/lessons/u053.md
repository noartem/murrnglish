The office plant has been dying for weeks. Nobody wants to admit they forgot it. On Friday Ravi finally speaks.
> Ravi: I offered ==to water== it, but honestly I kept ==forgetting==.
> Marta: Well, ==admit== you forgot. We all did.
> Ravi: ==I denied== saying anything, which was true.
> Marta: You ==suggested buying== a new one on Monday.
> Ravi: I did. And now I've ==given up trying== to save this one.

## The -ing family
A large group of verbs takes **-ing** and never **to**. You will meet more of them all the time, so learn them in small families rather than one by one.

::: words
enjoy · finish · avoid · mind · risk · consider · imagine · suggest · admit · deny · keep · practise · postpone
:::

::: rule 🧩 the shape
verb + **-ing**

_I enjoy **reading**. / She suggested **going**. / They mind **waiting**._

Whatever follows keeps its own object: _enjoy **reading novels**_, not _read novels_.
:::

::: examples
- She suggested ==going== to the zoo.
- He didn't fancy ==playing tennis==.
- They didn't mind ==waiting== a few minutes.
- Have you finished ==reading== the paper?
- I can't help ==laughing== at that joke.
- He denied ==causing== the accident.
:::

## The verbs that describe an action in progress
A few of these talk about something you did **again and again**, or **stopped** doing:

::: form
# 🔁 the pattern
verb + **-ing** + the moment
keep doing | _I keep **forgetting** things._
put off doing | _I've put off **paying** this bill._
stop doing | _It stopped **raining**._
go on doing | _He went on **talking**._
:::

**Stop** is worth a second look, because it has both meanings and they are opposites:

::: compare
# ⏸️ stop doing — the action ends
- Please stop ==asking== me questions!
- It stopped ==raining== at last.
# ▶️ stop to do — pause one thing, do another
- It stopped ==to let a train pass==.

## Admitting, denying, suggesting
Three verbs that describe **what somebody said or decided**:

::: cards
# 👥 admit
"I locked the file." — She ==admitted locking== it.
# 🙈 deny
"I sent it." — He ==denied sending== it.
# 💡 suggest
"Let's get a taxi." — He ==suggested getting== a taxi.
# 🧠 admit + not
"I didn't cheat." — She ==admitted not cheating==.
:::

**Admit** also takes the negative directly: _She admitted **not telling** the truth._

## before, without, by
**-ing** also follows prepositions, and it is often a shorter way to say a purpose clause — see [[u64|to, for and so that]].

::: examples
- I left ==without saying== goodbye.
- She got the job ==by taking== the exam early.
- Instead of ==paying== rent, he bought the flat.
- He apologised ==for forgetting== her name.
:::

::: trap
✗ ~~I enjoy to read.~~
✓ I enjoy ==reading==.
✗ ~~She suggested to go.~~
✓ She suggested ==going==.
✗ ~~He denied to cause the accident.~~
✓ He denied ==causing== the accident.
✗ ~~I put off to pay the bill.~~
✓ I've put off ==paying== the bill.
✗ ~~I've given up to learn Japanese.~~
✓ I've given up ==trying== to learn Japanese.
With these verbs, **to** before another verb is wrong — except when the -ing clause itself
contains a **to**: _suggested **going** to a party_.
:::

::: note 🔁
The one safe test: if the verb is on the list, keep **-ing**. If you are not sure, say the whole sentence out loud — _I enjoy to walk?_ sounds wrong to every English speaker, while _I like to walk_ is fine, because **like** takes both.
:::

::: summary
- Many verbs take **-ing**, never **to**: enjoy, mind, finish, avoid, risk, suggest, admit,
  deny, keep, consider, imagine.
- **keep / put off / go on + -ing** describe something you did again and again or kept doing.
- **stop doing** ends an action; **stop to do** pauses to do something else.
- **admit / deny / suggest + -ing** report what someone said or decided.
- **-ing** also follows prepositions: without doing, by taking, instead of paying.
:::
