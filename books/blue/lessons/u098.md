::: scene 🍽️ The birthday
Two friends are trying to be polite about a party that went badly wrong.
> Dana: The food was really disappointing.
> Reece: It was the long wait that got me. I was so bored.
> Dana: The children were excited about the cake, though.
:::

## The thing, or the person?

An adjective in **-ing** describes the thing. An adjective in **-ed** describes the person who feels something because of that thing.

::: compare
# 📦 the thing causes it
- a ==boring== lecture
- a ==disappointing== film
- an ==exciting== trip

# 🙋 the person feels it
- a ==bored== child
- a ==disappointed== audience
- an ==excited== learner
:::

Ask: **is the sentence about the thing, or about the person?** _The film was boring_ is a judgement of the film. _We were bored_ is a report of an experience.

## One word, two faces

:::: row
::: compare
# 🟢 -ing: the thing
- The kitchen was ==disgusting==.
- The lecture was so ==confusing== that nobody asked anything.

# 🔴 -ed: the people in it
- We were ==disgusted by== the kitchen. (not _the kitchen was disgusted_)
- The students were ==confused by== the lecture.
:::

::: trap
✗ The lecture was bored.
✓ The lecture was ==boring==.
✗ The children were exciting about the cake.
✓ The children were ==excited about== the cake.
✗ I was surprising by the news.
✓ I was ==surprised by== the news.
:::
::::

## The three-step pattern

The same sentence often walks through all three: a **person** feels something → the **thing** does it → the **person** feels it again.

::: form
# 🧍 person
Donna ==teaches young children==. It is a hard job.

# 😓 the thing is tiring
The job is ==exhausting==.

# 😴 person again
She is often ==exhausted== at the end of the day.
:::
## Quiz

::: quiz
? The lecture lasted three hours. It was ___.
= boring
? We sat through it for three hours. We were ___.
= bored
? I couldn’t believe the price. I was ___.
= surprised
? Ten pounds for a sandwich is ___.
= surprising
? The children played in the garden all morning. They were ___.
= excited
? The teacher’s explanation was ___ and we understood nothing.
= confusing
? Don’t get ___ — the film is only ninety minutes.
= bored
? Getting up at five in the winter is ___ .
= exhausting
:::

::: summary
- **-ing** describes the thing; **-ed** describes the person who feels something about it.
- The sentence tells you which: the film was ==boring== — that judges the film, we were ==bored== — that reports the audience.
- The same word often does both: the news is **surprising**, we are **surprised**.
- The full pattern is person → thing → person: the job is **exhausting**, she is **exhausted**.
:::
