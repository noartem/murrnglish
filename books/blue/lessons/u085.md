::: scene 🏪 At the corner shop
The shopkeeper is serving Sofia, who is holding a basket.
> Sofia: Do you have ==any== oranges left?
> Shopkeeper: We've only got four. And ==somebody== is waiting at the till, by the way.
> Sofia: Fine. ==Anything== that doesn't need cooking would do.
> Shopkeeper: How about going out? ==Anywhere== warm and sunny is better than here.
:::

## The easy rule

**some** in positive sentences, **any** in negatives and in most questions.

::: compare
# ✅ some
- We bought ==some flowers==.
- The builders are behind schedule; they have ==some work== to catch up on.
- There's ==somebody== at the door.
- I want ==something== to eat.
# ❌ any
- We didn't buy ==any flowers==.
- He never does ==any work==.
- There isn't ==anybody== at the door.
- I don't want ==anything== to eat.
:::

## Questions: it depends what you know

**some** in a question when you expect the answer **yes** — because the thing exists, or you're asking for or offering it.

::: cards
# 🤔 expecting yes
- ==Is somebody coming to meet us?== (I expect so)
- ==Can I have some sugar, please?== (I can see the sugar)
- ==Would you like something to drink?== (I know there's some)
# 🤷 no idea
- ==Do you have any luggage?==
- ==Is there anybody in the house?==
:::

**any** can also stand in for a negative idea: _She left without any money_ = she took none.

## It doesn't matter which

**any** in the sense of **whichever you like**.

::: examples
- Take ==any train== — every one stops at Milton.
- Come and see me ==any time== you like.
- ==Anybody== could have come in — the door was unlocked.
- I could eat ==anything== tonight. I'm not fussy.
:::

## Somebody, anybody and they

These words are **singular**, even when they mean several people. English then uses **they / them / their**.

::: examples
- ==Somebody== has left their umbrella behind.
- If ==anybody== objects, they can say so now.
:::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? Would you like ___ to drink?
= something
? We haven't got ___ milk left.
= any
? ___ parked here will be towed away.
= Anybody
? I don't mind. ___ will do.
= Anything
? Let's go ___ warm and sunny.
= somewhere
:::

::: summary
- **some** positive, **any** negative and in most questions.
- In questions, **some** when you expect yes or you are asking for something.
- **any** meaning "it doesn't matter which": _any bus, any time, anybody_.
- somebody/anyone are singular, so **they / them / their** follow.
:::
