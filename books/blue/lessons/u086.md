## no, none or any

**no + noun** = _not a, not any_. **none** stands alone for _no amount, not one_.

::: compare
# ❌ no + noun
- The road was closed for the fair, so there were ==no shops== open.
- I don't have ==no idea== what to do. (not natural)
# ✅ any in the negative
- I've got ==no change== for the ticket machine.
- There weren't ==any taxis==, so we walked.
# ✅ none on its own
- 'How many eggs are left?' '==None==.'
- All the tickets have gone. There are ==none== left.
# ✅ none of
- ==None of this money== is mine.
- ==None of them== came to the meeting.
:::

::: note 🔁
_I have no luggage._ · _'How much luggage do you have?' 'None.'_ · _I don't have any._ — all the same idea.

After **none of + plural**, singular or plural is both fine: _None of the students **were** happy_ / _**was** happy_.
:::

## nothing, nobody, nowhere

These are the negatives of _anything, anyone, anywhere_.

::: form
# 🚫 negative words
nothing = not anything
nobody / no-one = not anybody
nowhere = not anywhere

They can lead a sentence or stand alone as the answer:
'What's happening?' '==Nobody knows==.'
'Where are you off to?' '==Nowhere. I'm staying in==.'
:::

With them the verb is **positive**: _She said nothing_, never ~~didn't say nothing~~.

After nobody, English uses **they / them**: _Nobody did their homework_, _No-one told me the time._

## no- or any-

::: compare
# 🚫 the negative
- I don't want ==anything== to drink.
- ==Nobody== complained.
- ==Nowhere== is open.
- The town hasn't ==changed==.
# 🤷 it doesn't matter which
- We can go ==anywhere== you like.
- ==Anyone== is welcome.
- Take ==whatever== you need.
:::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? There are ___ taxis. We'll have to walk.
= no
? 'How much bread is left?' '___ .'
= None
? I don't want ___ to eat — I'm not hungry.
= anything
? ___ said anything about the delay.
= Nobody
? Let me know if you need ___ .
= anything
:::

::: summary
- **no + noun** = _not a, not any_; **none** stands alone; **none of** takes a group.
- **nothing, nobody, nowhere** = not anything / anybody / anywhere, with a positive verb.
- After **nobody** use they / them.
- **any-** in the negative, **any-** again when you mean "it doesn't matter which".
:::
