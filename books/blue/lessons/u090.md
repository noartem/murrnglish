## everybody, everything, all

**everybody / everyone / everything** are singular words, so they take a **singular verb**.

::: compare
# 👥 everybody / everyone
- ==Everybody was== delighted. (not _were_)
- ==Everyone has== finished. (not _have_)
- But: ==Everybody said they== enjoyed themselves.

# 📦 everything
- ==Everything went== wrong.
- ==Everything is== in the wrong place.

# ✅ all
all my friends · all of us · we were all happy
He knows all about cars.
All I've had today is soup.
:::

## all or whole?

**whole** = complete, and works mostly with **singular** nouns. **all** takes the plural or an uncountable noun.

::: compare
# 📕 whole = the entire thing
- Did you read ==the whole book==?
- She has worked ==the whole morning==.
- I ate ==a whole packet== of biscuits.

# 📦 all = the lot
- Have you used ==all the money== I handed you? (not _the whole money_)
- Go through ==all the information== slowly. (not _the whole information_)
:::

**whole** goes after the same words as _the / my / a_: _the whole book, my whole life, a whole day._

## every day, all day

::: form
# 🔁 every = how often
every day · every ten minutes · every six months
every time I see him, he looks tired.

# 🕐 all = from start to end
all day · all week · all evening
all the time (= always)
:::

Compare: _Every time I visit_, something new happens. _They visit all the time_ — meaning constantly.

::: trap
✗ She is exhausted. She has been on her feet ==all the day==.
✓ She is exhausted. She has been on her feet ==all day==.
✗ We went there ==all days== except Sunday.
✓ We went there ==every day== except Sunday.
✗ She works on ==each Monday==.
✓ She works ==every Monday==.
:::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? Nobody was hurt. ___ has their faults.
= Everybody
? ___ I’ve eaten today is a banana.
= All
? Did you read ___ book, or just part of it?
= the whole
? There’s a bus ___ ten minutes.
= every
? ___ went home early. The rest stayed.
= Some
:::

::: summary
- **everybody / everyone / everything** are singular: _Everybody was late_.
- After everybody, use they / them for the individuals.
- **whole** = complete, mostly singular: _the whole book_. **all** = the lot: _all the money_.
- **every** = how often (_every day_); **all** = the whole period (_all day_).
:::
