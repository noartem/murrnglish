::: scene 👀 Four windows on the same street
Four people have each seen part of the same minute, and no two accounts agree.
> Bea: I saw a van ==pulling up== outside the pharmacy.
> Raj: No — I saw it ==pull up== and leave again. That was on purpose.
> Bea: You couldn't see the driver. I could hear him ==shouting== at someone.
> Raj: My neighbour said she watched two people ==loading== a crate.
> Bea: Nobody saw a balloon ==go up==. Raj, your account is useless.
> Raj: A cat sat on the wall ==staring== at all of us the entire time.
:::

## The two shapes
**See**, **hear**, **watch**, **notice** and **smell** each take two forms, and the choice answers
one question: **did you watch it right through, or catch a piece of it?**

::: form
# 👀 what you saw
see somebody + **verb** | the whole action, start to finish
see somebody + **-ing** | the action part-way through

_I saw him **hand** her the parcel._ — I watched him do it.
_I saw him **handing** her the parcel._ — that was the moment I caught.

_the whole action_ · _a snapshot_
:::

::: examples
- I saw her ==take== a photograph.
- I saw her ==taking== photographs all afternoon.
- A van ==pulled up== outside, then drove off.
- We heard a van ==pulling up== outside, then it drove off.
- I watched him ==climb== the wall.
- I watched him ==climbing== the wall, badly.
:::

The **-ing** form is the natural one for a moment of **surprise** or **reaction**. The plain verb
suits a plain report of what happened.

::: compare
# 🎬 the whole thing, watched
- ==I watched her pack== the cases.
- ==She saw the balloon go up== from the field.
# ⏱️ caught in the middle
- ==I watched her packing== the cases.
- ==She saw the balloon going up==.
:::

## hear and watch behave the same way
**Hear** follows the identical pattern, and **smell** joins it.

::: form
# 👃 hearing and smelling
hear somebody + **verb** | I heard her ==sing== the whole song.
hear somebody + **-ing** | I heard her ==singing== in the kitchen.
smell something + **-ing** | I could smell something ==burning==.
:::

::: examples
- I heard him ==shout== at the children.
- I heard him ==shouting== at the children.
- We watched the children ==play== in the garden.
- We watched them ==playing== in the garden.
- I could smell something ==burning== in the hall.
:::

## The action in its own right
Sometimes the verb that follows isn't about the seeing at all — it reports **what happened next**.
It stays plain, and the sense changes with it.

::: examples
- I saw two people ==loitering== by the gate. // I don't know who they were.
- I watched the cyclist ==swerve== and nearly go down.
- I thought I heard somebody ==murmur== something, so I turned round.
- Everybody heard the balloon ==burst==.
- I felt something ==crawling== up my sleeve.
:::

::: note 🧠
Two people **standing** there is a picture in front of you. A cyclist **swerve** is a sequence of
events — you were watching it happen.
:::

::: trap
✗ ~~I saw him handed her the parcel.~~
✓ I saw him ==hand== her the parcel. / I saw him ==handing== her the parcel.
✗ ~~I saw her to take a picture.~~
✓ I saw her ==take== a picture. / I saw her ==taking== a picture.
✗ ~~I heard him to shout at the children.~~
✓ I heard him ==shout==. / I heard him ==shouting==.
✗ ~~I watched him climb and then he fell.~~
✓ I watched him ==climbing== — then he fell.
There is never a **to** after **see / hear / watch** in this pattern.
:::

::: cards
# 🎬 the whole action
_I saw her ==lock== the door._
# ⏱️ in progress
_I saw her ==locking== the door._
# 👃 the senses
_I could smell something ==burning==._
# 🔗 what happened next
_I heard somebody ==drop== something._
:::

::: summary
- **see / hear / watch / notice / smell + verb** = the whole action.
- **+ -ing** = the action caught part-way; natural for surprise and reaction.
- The plain form when the following verb is a separate event: the action happens, then you see it.
- No **to** after these verbs: _I saw him **hand** her the parcel._
:::
