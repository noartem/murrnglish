## When I do: the present for the future
After **when, as soon as, before, after, until, while, if**, use the **present** for a future meaning. The other half of the sentence can have _will_.

:::: row
::: rule 🔗 time word + present
I'll call you ==when== I ==get== home.

==As soon as== the rain ==stops==, we'll leave.

Wait here ==until== I ==come== back.
:::
::: form
# 🧩 future part + time part
I'll make tea | when you ==come==
We'll start | as soon as Jo ==arrives==
Finish your homework | before you ==go== out
:::
::::

::: trap
✗ I'll tell you when I ~~will know~~.
✓ I'll tell you when I know.
✗ Wait until the light ~~will change~~.
✓ Wait until the light changes.
:::

::: examples
- What do you want to be ==when== you ==grow up==?
- I'll feel calmer ==after== the exam ==is== over.
- Shall we eat ==before== the film ==starts==?
- ==If== it =='s== sunny tomorrow, we'll have a picnic.
:::

## When I've done: finished first
Use the **present perfect** after these words to stress that one thing will be **complete** before the other happens.

::: compare
# ⏱️ when I do
- ==When== I ==see== Ali, I'll give him the key.
- both things at about the same time
# ✅ when I've done
- ==When== I=='ve read== the book, I'll lend it to you.
- first finish reading, then lend
:::

::: cards
# 🧽 the dishes
You can watch TV ==after== you=='ve done== the dishes.
# 🎓 the course
==Once== I=='ve passed== my test, I'll buy a car.
# 🙈 the apology
I won't talk to him ==until== he=='s said== sorry.
:::

## When or if?
**when** = you are sure it will happen. **if** = maybe it will, maybe it won't.

::: compare
# 📅 when — it will happen
- I'm going to the shop. I'll buy bread ==when== I'm there.
- I'll call you ==when== I get back on Sunday.
# 🎲 if — it might happen
- ==If== the shop's open, I'll buy bread.
- ==If== I don't see you tomorrow, I'll call you.
:::

::: quiz
? I'll wait here until you ___ (finish).
= finish / 've finished
? As soon as I ___ (hear) any news, I'll let you know.
= hear / 've heard
? We're meeting at six. ___ I see Pia, I'll tell her. (when / if)
= When
? Take an umbrella. ___ it rains, you'll need it. (when / if)
= If
:::

::: summary
- After _when, as soon as, before, after, until, if_ → **present**, not _will_.
- Use the **present perfect** there when one action must be finished first.
- **when** = sure to happen; **if** = possible.
:::
