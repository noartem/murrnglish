Something was burnt, and somebody said so.
> Rosa: I'm sorry ==for== the smoke alarm. I lost track of the time.
> Hal: Don't be sorry ==about== it. The chicken was lovely.
> Rosa: You're too generous ==to me==. I nearly ruined the evening.
> Hal: I'm just glad you were honest ==with me==. I was worried ==about== the food.

> Hal: It was kind ==of you== to try at all.

## An adjective judging a person: of or to

**of** goes with the person being judged; **to** with the person the judgement is aimed at.

::: compare
# 🎁 of — the person who acted
- It was kind ==of you== to wait for me.
- That was careless ==of him==, not of me.
# 👉 to — the person it was aimed at
- They have always been kind ==to me==.
- Why are you rude ==to strangers==?
:::

==== Never ~~kind with me~~ in this meaning. But **kind to** and **kind of** say different things, and the meaning decides.

## Feelings about things and people

::: compare
# 😠 angry — three shapes
- There's no point getting angry ==about== things that don't matter.
- Are you annoyed ==with us== for missing the meeting?
- They're furious ==with us== ==for making== such a noise.
# 😟 worried, excited, happy — about
- She's worried ==about== the interview.
- They're excited ==about== the trip.
- I'm happy ==about== the new job.
# 😄 pleased, satisfied — with
- They were delighted ==with== the present.
- Are you happy ==with== your marks?
:::

**Angry at** also exists for a thing or a person: _angry at the delay, angry at him._

## Surprised and impressed

**at** and **by** are both possible after **surprised / shocked / amazed**. **impressed** takes **with** or **by**.

::: compare
# 😮 surprised
- Everyone was surprised ==at the news==.
- Nobody was shocked ==by the news==.
# 👍 impressed
- I'm very impressed ==with her English==.
- or impressed ==by her English==.
::::

## fed up, tired, sorry

::: form
# 😪 fed up / bored with something
fed up with my job
bored with the film
# 😑 tired of something
tired of waiting
tired of this weather
:::

**Sorry** has three separate uses, and they are easy to mix up.

::: compare
# 📝 sorry about — a situation
- I'm sorry ==about the paint on the wall==. I'll repaint it.
- Sorry ==about last night==.
# 🙋 sorry for — what you did
- I'm sorry ==for shouting at you==.
- Sorry ==for the rain==. ==(or sorry about the rain)==
# 💛 sorry for somebody — you feel sorry for them
- I feel sorry ==for Mark==. He's had such bad luck.
:::

==== ~~I feel sorry about Mark~~ is wrong: you are sorry **for** a person in a bad situation. And for what *you* did, say **I'm sorry I shouted at you.**

::: summary
- **of** with the person being judged, **to** with the person it is aimed at: _kind of you, kind to me._
- Feelings about a thing take **about**; about a person **with**: _angry about the noise, angry with me._
- **at** or **by** after **surprised**; **with** or **by** after **impressed**.
- **sorry about** a situation, **sorry for** what you did, **sorry for** someone you feel sorry for.
:::
