## much or many

**much** with uncountable nouns, **many** with plurals.

::: form
# 📦 uncountable → much
much luck · much time · much water · much money

# 👥 plural → many
many friends · many people · many cars · many children
:::

**a lot of / lots of / plenty of** work with both.

## Not much, not many

**little** = not much. **few** = not many. The difference is the noun, not the idea.

::: compare
# 📉 little / few — not enough
- Gary is swamped; he has ==little time== for other things.
- Vicky doesn't stay in touch; she has ==few friends== there.
- The rain has been poor. We've had ==little rain==.
- Few people came, and most left early.
# 🙂 a little / a few — some, and enough
- We've got ==a little time== before the train.
- I've got ==a few friends== here — come and meet them.
- Can I give you ==a little advice==?
- We only walked ==a few miles==.
:::

**only a little**, **only a few** — not ~~only little~~.

## much is rare in positives

::: trap
✗ We spent ==much== money on that holiday.
✓ We spent ==a lot of== money on that holiday.
✗ I see David ==much== these days.
✓ I see David ==a lot== these days.
:::

But **too much** and **so much** are perfectly normal: _You spend too much on coffee._

**many** is fine in every kind of sentence: _Cyclists here ride badly._ / _Do you know many cyclists?_ / _There aren't many visitors in winter._

And we say _many years_, not ~~a lot of years~~.

## plenty of, plenty to

::: form
# 💰 plenty of + noun
There's ==plenty of time== — no need to hurry.
He has ==plenty of money== now.
There are ==plenty of hotels== in town.

# 🎯 plenty to + verb
There's ==plenty to do== here in the evenings.
She still has ==plenty to learn==.
:::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? Did you do ___ work this week?
= much
? There aren't ___ tourists in January.
= many
? I've only got ___ minutes before the train leaves.
= a few
? She has ___ patience with small children.
= a little
? There's ___ to see at the fair.
= plenty
:::

::: summary
- **much** + uncountable, **many** + plural; **a lot of** works with both.
- **little / few** mean "not enough"; **a little / a few** mean "some".
- **too much / so much** are fine in positives; plain **much** is not.
- **plenty of** + noun, **plenty to** + verb.
:::
