## Have / has + past participle
The **present perfect** joins a past action to the present. You build it from **have / has** and the verb's **past participle** — the _-ed_ form for regular verbs, a form of its own for many others.

:::: row
::: form
# ➕ / ➖
I / you / we / they | ==have== / ==haven't== | fixed
he / she / it | ==has== / ==hasn't== | fixed
# ❓
==have== | I / you / we / they | fixed?
==has== | he / she / it | fixed?
:::
::: words 🔀 Participles to know
lose → lost · break → broken · grow → grown · shrink → shrunk · go → gone · see → seen · forget → forgotten · do → done · find → found · come → come
:::
::::

::: note ✂️
In speech, **have** and **has** shrink: I've, she's, they've, it's. _It's stopped_ here means _it has stopped_.
:::

## A past action with a result now
You use it when the action is in the past, but what matters is how things are **now**.

::: compare One action, a result you can see
# 🕰️ Before
- The vase was on the shelf.
- My phone was in my pocket.
- The shop was open until 6.
# 👀 Now
- The cat ==has broken== the vase.
- I=='ve lost== my phone. I can't call you.
- It's 6.15. The shop ==has closed==.
:::

It's also the tense for **changes**: something was one way, and now it's different.

::: cards
# 📈 going up
The price of coffee ==has gone up== again.
# 🌱 growing
Look how tall your son ==has grown==!
# ✨ getting better
Your guitar playing ==has improved== a lot.
:::

::: timeline
point past | the price rose
span past now | it's still higher
point now | you notice it
:::

## Just, already, yet
Three small words that love this tense.

:::: row
::: rule ⏱️ just and already
**just** = a very short time ago: I=='ve just== woken up.

**already** = sooner than expected: The film has ==already== started!

Both go between _have_ and the participle.
:::
::: rule ⏳ yet
**yet** = until now, and you expect it to happen. It goes at the **end** of questions and negatives.

Have you replied ==yet==? — No, I haven't decided what to say ==yet==.
:::
::::

::: examples
- Has the parcel arrived ==yet==? — Yes, it=='s just== come.
- I can't come with you. I haven't packed ==yet==.
- Don't tell Ruth the news. She ==already knows==. // already also fits the present simple
:::

::: note 🇺🇸
In American English you'll also hear the past simple here: _I just ate. Did you call her yet?_ Both are fine; this course uses the present perfect.
:::

## Been or gone?
_Go_ has two participles, and they tell different stories.

::: compare
# ✈️ gone
- Ana ==has gone== to Lisbon.
- She's there now, or on the way.
# 🔙 been
- Ana ==has been== to Lisbon.
- She went and came back.
:::

::: trap
✗ Where's Leo? — He's ~~been~~ to the gym. He'll be home at eight.
✓ He's gone to the gym. He'll be home at eight.
✗ Hi! I've just ~~gone~~ to the bakery. Want a croissant?
✓ I've just been to the bakery.
:::

::: quiz
? My keys aren't in my bag. I ___ (lose) them.
= have lost / 've lost
? ___ (you / finish) your homework yet?
= Have you finished
? We're too late — the train ___ (already / leave).
= has already left
? Is Sam here? — No, he's ___ home. (been / gone)
= gone
:::

::: summary
- **have / has + past participle**: a past action seen from now.
- Use it for results you can see now and for changes.
- **just** and **already** sit in the middle; **yet** ends questions and negatives.
- **gone** = still away; **been** = went and came back.
:::
