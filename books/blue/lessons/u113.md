hook: 🌧️ "It rained all day. We had a good time." Two facts, one concession.
goals: join two facts with although or in spite of · use despite with a noun or -ing · put though at the end of a sentence · tell concession from reason
---

::: scene 🌧️ A wet holiday
Two people keep saying the same thing in different words.
It rained the whole time. We enjoyed ourselves.
==Although it rained== the whole time, we enjoyed ourselves.
==In spite of the rain== we enjoyed ourselves.
:::

## although

**although** joins two **clauses**. Each half has its own subject and verb. Put it at the start of the sentence with a comma after it, or in the middle with commas round it.

:::: row
::: compare
# 🟢 clause + clause
- ==Although I hadn't studied==, I passed the exam.
- I passed the exam ==although I hadn't studied==.
- ==Although the job sounds enormous==, the pay is poor.

# 🟢 after although: subject + verb
- Although ==it was quite cold==, nobody complained.
- Sarah wasn't wearing a coat ==although it was cold==.
:::

::: note ✂️
**although** never takes a clause after **in spite of**: not _in spite of it was raining_.

:::
::::

## in spite of and despite

These take a **noun**, a **pronoun** or an **-ing** form — never a clause.

::: compare
# ✅ noun, pronoun, -ing
- ==In spite of the rain== we went walking.
- ==Despite it== we went walking.
- ==Despite being tired== she finished the race.
- ==In spite of what he said== I still went.

# ❌ a clause
- ✗ _In spite of it was raining_
- ✗ _Despite the rain was heavy_
:::

**despite** is the same word with no **of**: _despite the rain_, never _despite of the rain_.

::: trap
✗ In spite of all our careful plans, nothing worked.
✓ ==Although we had planned everything carefully==, nothing worked.
✗ She was late because of the bus was late.
✓ She was late ==because the bus was late==.
✗ Despite of the rain we went out.
✓ ==Despite the rain== we went out.
:::

## even though and though

**even though** is the same as **although**; the **even** just makes the contrast stronger. **though** is a shorter, everyday word, and in speech it often drops to the end of the sentence.

::: compare
# 🔁 a bit stronger
- ==Even though I was exhausted==, I carried on.

# 🗣️ short, and dropped to the end
- The house isn't very nice. I like the garden ==though==.
- We pass in the street daily. He has never said hello ==though==.
:::

## Concession or reason?

**although** and **in spite of** fight against the fact. **because** agrees with it — and that changes the meaning.

::: compare
# 🙅 fighting the fact
- We played tennis ==although the wind was bitter==.
- ==In spite of the rain== we went out.

# 👍 agreeing with the fact
- We stayed indoors ==because the wind was bitter==.
:::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? ___ the heating was on, the room was cold.
= Although
? Sarah wasn't wearing a coat ___ it was quite cold.
= although
? In ___ of the delay we still got there.
= spite
? ___ my foot hurt, I walked home.
= Even though
? The film was dull ___ I enjoyed it.
= though
? We liked the village ___ of the weather.
= despite
? ___ she didn't speak the language well, she managed.
= Although
? He kept going ___ he was exhausted.
= though
:::

::: summary
- **although** joins two clauses; **in spite of** and **despite** take a noun, pronoun or -ing.
- **despite** has no **of**: _despite the rain_.
- **even though** = **although**, a little stronger; **though** alone is short and often ends the sentence.
- **because** agrees with the fact, **although** fights against it.
:::
