## A sentence folded inside another

A **relative clause** does one of two jobs: it tells the listener *which* person or thing you mean, or it adds a detail about one you both already know about. It follows the noun it talks about, and when it identifies the noun there is no comma.

That is what makes two sentences into one:

::: form
# ✂️ before
A burglar broke into the house.
The burglar stole my camera.

# 📎 after
The burglar ==who broke into the house== stole my camera.
:::

## who for people, which for things

::: compare
# 👤 people
- ==The woman who phoned== wants you to tell you something.
- ==The boy that lives next door== is called Femi.

# 📦 things
- ==The shop which sells bread== shuts at six.
- ==The film that we watched== was dull.
:::

**that** is the neutral word: it goes with people *and* things, and in speech it is never the wrong choice.

::: note 🎯
Reach for **who** when you mean a person, **which** when you mean a thing, and **that** whenever you would rather not think about it.
:::

## What is the clause doing?

Sometimes the clause is the one doing the action, so it stands in for a subject. Sometimes it only takes the object. Ask yourself: who did it?

::: compare
# 🏃 the clause is the subject
- ==The man who called== was never rude.
- ==Something that was stolen== is in my bag.

# 🎒 the clause is the object
- I know ==the woman who called==.
- He watched ==a film that was dull==.
:::

In both, the word **who / which / that** stays in. What moves is the noun it belongs to: that noun is the real subject or the real object.

::: examples
- Something ==that rang== in the house. (something rang)
- I know a woman ==who sings== in the opera. (she sings)
- Nobody saw ==the car which crashed==. (the car crashed)
:::

## Where the clause stands

::: form
# 🧩 after the noun
The girl ==whose bag was stolen== is crying.
a problem ==that nobody noticed== — a problem nobody noticed

# 🧱 or it can come first
==A man who can cook== is worth his weight in gold.
:::

A clause can describe a person, a thing, a place, even a whole idea: we had a day that we will never forget.

::: trap
✗ The film who we watched had no sound.
✓ The film ==that/which== we watched had no sound.
✗ She married a man which is a dentist.
✓ She married a man ==who/that== is a dentist.
✗ That is the road what leads down to the harbour.
✓ That is the road ==that/which== leads down to the harbour.
:::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? The woman ___ phoned says she is a journalist.
= who
? That’s the café ___ we had lunch on Sunday.
= which
? I can’t find the book ___ I left on the train.
= that
? We hired a man ___ to paint the roof.
= who
? Two roads lead out of the village. This is the one ___ is closed.
= that
? ___ says otherwise is wrong: the meeting is at nine.
= Whoever
? Look at the man ___ is standing by the door.
= who
:::

::: summary
- A **relative clause** identifies a noun or adds a detail: the man ==who called== was late.
- **who** for people, **which** for things, **that** for both.
- The clause goes right after its noun, and **who / that / which** stays in — leaving it out is unit 93.
- The clause can be the subject (the man ==who stole it==) or the object (the man ==that I met==).
:::
