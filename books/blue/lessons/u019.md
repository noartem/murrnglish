## Arranged plans: I'm doing
Use the **present continuous** for something you have already fixed: you've booked it, agreed it with someone, put it in the diary.

:::: row
::: rule 🗓️ present continuous = arranged
I=='m seeing== the dentist on Thursday.

We=='re having== friends over tonight.

The time is usually said: _tonight, on Friday, next week_.
:::
::: timeline
point 30 | Omar books the flight
point now | now
point 80 | Sunday: he's flying
:::
::::

::: cards
# 🎫 booked
Lena=='s going== to a concert on Saturday — she has the tickets.
# 🤝 agreed
Sam and I ==are meeting== for coffee after work.
# 🚫 not happening
I=='m not working== on Monday. It's a day off.
:::

::: note 💡
Questions about plans work the same way: _Where ==are== you ==staying==? How long ==are== you ==going== for? ==Is== Mia ==coming== too?_
:::

## Timetables: it leaves at…
Use the **present simple** for events fixed by a timetable or a programme: trains, flights, films, courses, shops.

::: examples
- The ferry ==leaves== at 7.15 and ==gets== to the island at 9.
- What time ==does== the match ==start==?
- The sale ==ends== on Sunday.
- Term ==starts== on 4 September.
:::

::: compare
# 👤 people's arrangements
- I=='m leaving== tomorrow at nine.
- ==Are== you ==coming== to the party?
- present continuous
# 🚆 timetables, programmes
- My bus ==leaves== at nine.
- When ==does== the party ==start==?
- present simple
:::

::: trap
✗ ~~I go~~ to the cinema with Zoe tonight.
✓ I'm going to the cinema with Zoe tonight.
✗ ~~Do you do~~ anything this weekend?
✓ Are you doing anything this weekend?
For your own arrangements, the present simple sounds like a timetable. Keep it for times that are printed somewhere.
:::

::: quiz
? ___ (you / do) anything on Saturday? — No, I'm free.
= Are you doing
? The film ___ (start) at 8.30, so let's eat first.
= starts
? Kim ___ (not / come) tonight. She's got a cold.
= isn't coming / 's not coming
? What time ___ (the shop / close) today?
= does the shop close
:::

::: summary
- **Present continuous** for things you have arranged: _I'm meeting Jo tonight._
- Ask about plans the same way: _What are you doing tomorrow?_
- **Present simple** for timetables and programmes: _The train leaves at 6._
- Don't use the present simple for personal arrangements.
:::
