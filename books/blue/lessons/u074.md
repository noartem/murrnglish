> Neighbour: My boy had a bad fall last week. He's been ==in hospital== ever since.

> Neighbour: Next week, they think. Then he'll be straight back at ==school==.

## The idea, or the building

Without **the**, these nouns mean the **general idea** — what the place is for.

With **the**, they mean **that particular building**, and you are walking into it.

::: compare
# 💭 the general idea
- Children go to ==school==. (= to be pupils)
- He's been ==in hospital==. (= as a patient)
- His brother is ==in prison==. (= serving a sentence)
- She goes to ==church== on Sundays.
- When I finish ==school== I want to study at ==university==.
# 🏛️ the actual building
- I went to ==the school== to see her teacher.
- Kate went to ==the hospital== to visit Joe.
- Two men went to ==the prison== after the break-in.
- Workmen went up to ==the church== to mend the roof.
:::

::: note 🔑
**Visitor** or **patient** → the building. **Student** or **prisoner** → the idea.
:::

## bed, work, home

These three drop **the** after their usual prepositions too.

::: form
# 🛏️ bed
go to ==bed== · be in ==bed==
but: sit down on ==the bed==

# 💼 work
go to ==work== · be at work · finish work at six · be off work
but: I love ==my work==

# 🏠 home
go ==home== · get ==home== · arrive ==home== · be at ==home==
but: a home page on the internet
:::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? After that long drive we got back ___ very late.
= home
? Tom usually finishes ___ at five o'clock.
= work
? I always read ___ for a while before I sleep.
= in bed
? Why aren't your children at ___ today? Are they ill?
= school
? Break the law and you could end up ___.
= in prison
? She left school and went on to ___.
= university
:::

::: summary
- No **the** for the general idea: _go to school, be in hospital, be in prison_.
- **the** for the building you walk into: _go to the school to see the teacher_.
- The same split applies to bed, work and home: _go to bed_ but _sit on the bed_.
:::
