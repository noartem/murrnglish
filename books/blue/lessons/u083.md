Ruth and her sister-in-law Salma are arguing about the shed.
> Salma: You've got plenty of room in ==your own== garage. Why keep everything at the allotment?
> Ruth: Because ==a friend of mine== gave me this greenhouse, and I grow all ==my own== tomatoes.
> Salma: Fair enough. Shall we fix the fence today?
> Ruth: Yes — I can't manage it ==by myself==, though.

## a friend of mine

**of mine, of yours, of hers** turns a noun back towards the speaker.

::: form
# 👥 of mine / yours / hers / ours / theirs
A ==friend of mine== is getting married on Saturday.

My sister knows the woman by the window — she's ==a friend of my sister's==.

Canceling early was ==a good idea of yours==.
:::

Not _a friend of me_. For a name, the apostrophe stays: _a friend of Tom's._

## my own

**my own** = only mine, not shared and not borrowed.

::: compare
# 🙋 my own
- I don't want to share a room. I want ==my own room==.
- Vicky and Gary would like ==their own house==.
- The flat hasn't got ==its own parking space==.
- It's ==my own fault== that I'm broke.
# ❌ not an own
- ✗ ==an own house==
- ✓ ==my own house==
:::

The **of my own** pattern works on longer phrases: a room of my own, problems of his own.

## own for doing it yourself

**own** also says that you do the job rather than pay or delegate.

::: examples
- Paul cuts ==his own hair==. (not at a barber)
- I'd like a garden so I can grow ==my own vegetables==.
- They write ==their own songs== instead of using someone else's.
:::

## Alone: two ways

::: form
# 🔄 on my own
on my own · on your own · on his own
on her own · on our own · on their own

# 🚶 by myself
by myself · by yourself · by himself
by herself · by ourselves · by themselves
:::

Either way you are saying she went without company — build it with on her own or with by herself.

::: trap
✗ Marisol is after ~~an own flat~~ of her own.
✓ Marisol is after ==her own flat==.
✗ I felt nervous, so I refused to go ~~by my own~~.
✓ I felt nervous, so I refused to go ==on my own==.
✗ Sam and Chris are colleagues ~~of me~~.
✓ Sam and Chris are colleagues ==of mine==.
:::

## Quiz

::: quiz
? Jason borrowed ___ from me.
= a book of mine
? She wants to start ___ .
= her own business
? Very young children shouldn't go swimming ___ .
= by themselves
? 'Who was Dan with when you saw him?' 'Nobody. He was ___ .'
= by himself
? My parents have travelled off with some friends ___ .
= of theirs
:::

::: summary
- **of mine / yours / hers / ours / theirs** — never _of me_.
- **my own** = only mine: _my own room, its own parking space_.
- **own** also means you do the work yourself: _he cuts his own hair_.
- **on my own** and **by myself** say the same thing: nobody else there.
:::
