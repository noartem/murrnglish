## A question inside a question
A **wh- question** can sit inside a bigger question. The outer question is polite — _Do you know…?_, _Could you tell me…?_ — and the inner one keeps its own word order.

::: compare
# ✓ natural
- ==Do you know what time the shop opens?==
- ==Could you tell me where the station is?==
- ==Do you remember why she left?==
# ✗ inverted twice
- ~~Do you know what time does the shop open?~~
- ~~Could you tell me where is the station?~~
- ~~Do you remember why did she leave?~~
:::

Inside the big question the subject stays **before** the verb, exactly as in a plain statement. Only the outer question inverts.

::: rule 🧩 one inversion
> **Do you know** + (wh- + subject + verb)
> **Could you tell me** + (wh- + subject + verb)
> **I wonder** + (wh- + subject + verb)

_Could you tell me **when the meeting starts**?_
:::

::: examples
- Do you know ==what this word means==?
- Do you know ==where Helen works==?
- I can't remember ==where I left the keys==.
- Do you know ==what time the doors open==?
:::

## The word order of a plain question
To put a question's words back in order, start from the **statement**, then move the auxiliary in front.

::: form
# 🧵 statement → question
Amy gets up early | Why **does Amy** get up early?
it is far to the airport | How far **is it** to the airport?
Tom is how old | I wonder how old **Tom is**
they have been married | How long **have they** been married?
:::

::: note 🧭
**How long have they been married?** asks about the **duration**. **How long do you live there?** asks about **length**. Same opening, different meaning — so check what you really want to know.
:::

## Reporting a question
When you tell somebody what another person asked, the question becomes a statement:

- **wh- questions** keep the question word and the normal word order: _He asked me **where I was staying**._
- **yes/no questions** need **if** or **whether**: _She asked **if I liked** the city._ Both are fine; **whether** is a little more formal.

::: examples
- “Where are you from?” → He asked me ==where I was from==.
- “Do you like Lima?” → She asked ==if I liked== Lima.
- “Have you been before?” → They asked ==whether I had been== before.
- “How long will you stay?” → He asked ==how long I was going to stay==.
:::

The tenses move back one step, because you're reporting words already spoken — that is [[u47|reported speech]] in one line.

::: trap
✗ ~~She asked me where was I staying.~~
✓ She asked me where ==I was staying==.
✗ ~~He asked me that where I was from.~~
✓ He asked me ==where== I was from.
✗ ~~They asked me that did I like it.~~
✓ They asked me ==if I liked== it.
Report a question as a statement — no inversion, no _that_ before a wh- word.
:::

::: cards
# 🗺️ wh- question
""Where is the hotel?" → He asked me where ==the hotel was==.
# ❓ yes/no question
"Did she pay?" → She asked ==if she had paid==.
# 🕰️ tenses move back
"How long have you lived here?" → He asked how long ==I had lived== there.
# 🚶 subject pronoun changes
"Are you ready?" → She asked ==if I was== ready.
:::

::: note 🪞
**you** in the question becomes **I / he / she** in your report, because now *you* are the one telling the story.

::: quiz
? Do you know what time ___?
= the doors open / it is
? Could you tell me where ___?
= the bus stop is
? He asked me how long ___.
= I had been working there
? She asked ___ I had paid the bill.

? I can't remember where ___.
= I put my keys
? They asked me why ___ late.

:::

::: summary
- A wh- question inside a bigger question keeps **subject before verb**.
- The outer question inverts once: **Do you know…?**, **Could you tell me…?**
- Reporting a wh- question: keep the question word, normal order — _asked me where I was_.
- Reporting a yes/no question: add **if** or **whether**.
- Reported tenses move back one step, and **you** becomes **I / he / she**.
:::
