> Bea: And the garden?
> Nadia: I nearly gave up. I'm ==good at baking==, not at ==weeding==.

> Nadia: Only because I kept ==stopping for coffee==.
> Bea: Are you ==looking forward to decorating== it?

## Preposition + -ing, where a verb used to be
In a lot of phrases an old **noun** has been replaced by a **preposition + -ing**. The shape is
always the same, whatever the verb underneath.

::: form
# 🧱 the shape
preposition + **-ing**

_He was **good at** baking._ (= he was a good baker)
_She apologised **for** being late._ (= she said sorry that she was late)
:::

::: words
good at · bad at · keen on · tired of · fed up with · frightened of · afraid of · capable of · instead of · fond of
:::

::: examples
- Helen is ==good at remembering== names.
- I'm ==tired of waiting== at airports.
- He's ==keen on cycling== rather than driving.
- She's ==capable of handling== the accounts alone.
:::

## Describing chances, trouble and effort
A second group sits with the **nouns** — *chance, problem, difficulty, trouble, opportunity* —
and takes **of** or nothing at all.

::: form
# 📊 nouns that want -ing
a chance **of winning** | a little chance of ==winning== the lottery
a problem **remembering** | a problem ==remembering== names
difficulty **understanding** | no difficulty ==understanding== each other
trouble **finding** | no trouble ==finding== a job
spend time **cleaning** | spend a lot of time ==cleaning==
:::

::: examples
- You have little chance ==of winning== it.
- She had no trouble ==finding== a job.
- We had no difficulty ==understanding== one another.
- I waste too much time ==watching== television.
- He spent the morning ==writing== emails.
:::

## by -ing — how it was done
**by + -ing** explains the **method**: how somebody managed it, or what caused it.

::: cards
# 🪜 by + -ing
_I reached the top shelf ==by standing on a chair==._
We made the room look better ==by putting pictures== on the walls.
He got into trouble ==by borrowing too much==.
# 🚫 without + -ing
_We saw the film ==without having to queue==._
She left ==without saying== goodbye.
# 🔄 instead of + -ing
_We went to a café ==instead of eating== at home._
:::

**By** says "this is how it was done". **Without** and **instead of** name what did **not**
happen.

## Looking forward to — a trap
**Looking forward to** is extremely common, and the **to** here is a **preposition**, not the start of
an infinitive. So what follows is a **noun** or **-ing** — never a bare verb.

::: compare
# ✗ a bare verb after look forward to
- I'm looking forward to ==meet== you.
# ✓ a noun or -ing
- I'm looking forward to ==meeting== you.
- I'm looking forward to ==the trip==.
:::

The same is true of these, which are prepositions wearing the costume of a verb:

::: words
look forward to · get used to · be used to · object to · be opposed to · be devoted to · when it comes to
:::

_We're looking forward to **hearing** from you._ / _I'm used to **working** nights._ / _She
objected to **being** interrupted._

::: trap
✗ ~~She is good at cook.~~
✓ She is good at ==cooking==.
✗ ~~I had a trouble of finding a job.~~
✓ I had ==trouble finding== a job.
✗ ~~I'm looking forward to see you.~~
✓ I'm looking forward to ==seeing== you.
✗ ~~He left without say goodbye.~~
✓ He left without ==saying== goodbye.
✗ ~~We did it by paint the wall.~~
✓ We did it ==by painting== the wall.
After a preposition the verb always takes **-ing** — even when that preposition is spelled
**to**, as in **looking forward to** and **used to**.
:::

::: note 🔁
A quick test: put **the** before the word after the preposition. If **the** fits, it is a
noun — _used to **the** noise_ — and **-ing** follows. If it doesn't, it is **-ing** by rule
either way.
:::

::: summary
- **preposition + -ing**: good at, tired of, keen on, instead of, without, by, afraid of.
- Nouns take **-ing** too: a chance of winning, no trouble finding, spend time cleaning.
- **by + -ing** says how it was done; **without / instead of + -ing** say what didn't happen.
- **looking forward to**, **used to**, **object to** are prepositions — **-ing** follows, never a
  bare verb.
:::
