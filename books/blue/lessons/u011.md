::: scene 🛋️ Meeting the neighbour
Nadia has just moved in. Her neighbour Pete brings her a plant.
> Nadia: Do you live alone?
> Pete: Yes. I ==have lived== here ==for== twelve years now.
> Nadia: And the dog? ==How long have you had== him?
> Pete: Biscuit? Since he was a puppy. He=='s been sleeping== on your doormat ==since== Monday, by the way.
:::

## Now — and since when?
To say something is true **now**, you use a present tense. To say **how long** it has been true, you need the present perfect.

::: compare
# 📍 just now
- Pete ==lives== on this street.
- Nadia ==is learning== Greek.
- Are you ==married==?
# 📏 now + how long
- He=='s lived== here for twelve years.
- She=='s been learning== Greek since June.
- How long ==have you been== married?
:::

::: trap
✗ I ~~know~~ her since we were kids.
✓ I've known her since we were kids.
✗ How long ~~are you~~ in Paris?
✓ How long have you been in Paris?
:::

## Simple or continuous?
For actions and activities, either works, and **have been doing** is more common. For states, only the simple form is possible.

:::: row
::: rule 🔄 activities
How long ==have you been working== here?

How long ==have you worked== here?

Both fine.
:::
::: rule 🧱 states
**be, know, have** (own), **like**: simple only.

We=='ve known== each other for ages.

She=='s had== a cold all week.
:::
::::

::: note 🏡
With **live** and **work**, the continuous often sounds more temporary: _I've been living with friends since May_ (it's not permanent). With **always**, use the simple form: I=='ve always lived== by the sea.
:::

## For ages: the negative
To say something hasn't happened during a period up to now, use **haven't / hasn't + past participle**.

::: examples
- I ==haven't been== to a concert for months.
- Grace ==hasn't called== since Easter.
- We ==haven't eaten== out for ages. Let's go somewhere tonight!
:::

::: quiz
? Ola works in a bank. She ___ (work) there since 2019.
= 's been working / has been working / 's worked / has worked
? I ___ (know) Theo for about ten years.
= 've known / have known
? Look, Dad is asleep on the sofa. He ___ (be) asleep for an hour.
= 's been / has been
? Is Ruby married? — Yes, she ___ married to a pilot.
= 's / is
:::

::: summary
- True now → present: I live, she's married.
- How long until now → present perfect: I've lived, she's been married.
- Activities: **have been doing** or **have done**; states: **have done** only.
- **always** → simple; **haven't done … for ages** for a gap.
:::
